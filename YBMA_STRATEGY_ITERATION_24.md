# 1 · The wordmark — what the drawing is

### A literal rotation does not produce an A

The Session 20 Y rotated 180° gives a mast on two legs with no crossbar. **It reads as a lambda:
YBMΛ.** Session 20's objection is correct and was confirmed by drawing.

### Session 20's A — adopted, unchanged

A normal A with its crossbar (66), **whose flat apex is exactly one stem width (17) — the Y's stem.**
The word opens on a stem and closes on an apex of the same width. Session 20's own note: *quiet
strangeness — it looks normal, then doesn't.* **Nothing rotates. The silhouettes rhyme.**

### Configuration (Session 20 `TURN`, unaltered)

| | Value | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Stem | **17** | monoline; every terminal square |
| Y | 74 wide · fork 56 | arms at dx/dy 0.66 |
| B | 65 wide · waist 48 | elliptical bowls — the only curved letter |
| M | 88 wide · vertex 90 | the V stops 10 above the baseline |
| A | 74 wide · **apex 17** · bar 66 | legs at dx/dy 0.285 · apex = one stem |
| Tracking | 10 | **total 331 × 100** · minimum **20 px / 6 mm** |

**On record, not changed:** the Y's arms (0.66) and the A's legs (0.285) are on different angles, and
U3's descent (0.5625, derived from the Cut Y in Session 22) matches neither exactly. Close enough
that the family reads; **the guideline should say "approximately the Y's angle," not claim a
derivation.**

# 2 · U3 — unchanged, retested

Geometry identical to Session 23 (120 square · entry 18 · descent 0.5625 · turn 86 · joint 11 ·
band shear). Retested alone on the board so it stands on its own: ladder to 16 px · circular crop ·

The descent angle (0.5625) is **close to the Y's arms (0.66), not equal.** That near-match and the

# 3 · Lockup

**Primary:** symbol at cap height, 0.4 cap from the wordmark. With no tail, symbol and wordmark share
their top and bottom edges exactly — **the lockup is simpler than Cut's, and that simplicity is the
one thing Turned gains here.** **Secondary:** symbol above, centred — the wordmark's silhouette is
now symmetrical enough for centring to hold. Left-aligned stacking was tried and rejected: without
the tail's stem axis there is nothing to align to and it reads as an indent.

Rule of use unchanged: wordmark alone by default; lockup where the symbol is introduced beside the
name.

---

# 4 · Motion — the static form does not need it

**No turn animation.** Session 20's A is not a rotated Y; a rotation animation would show a form that
is not the A and then swap it — a trick, and the only trick in the identity. **Refused.** What the
mark supports is a reveal by cuts: Y, B, M, A at 140 ms intervals, no movement, no easing. Barely
motion, which is correct — the brief said the animation is subordinate to the identity, and this
identity is a still one. Motion lives in the system (transitions, the symbol as a fixed anchor), not
in the mark. The symbol does not animate.

# 5 · Endorsement — structural check

- **The symbol endorses what YBMA acquires** — *U3 · A YBMA COMPANY* — unchanged, and it was always

- **Divisions** can no longer sit on a line. The division name is set typographically: supporting

Subsidiary architecture is stage 8 and is not designed.

# 6 · Assessment — on the same terms as Session 23

### Excellent
- **The drawing is settled.** Founder-chosen, recovered exactly, nothing to refine. Monoline at stem
  17 is the most reproducible letterform there is.
- **The silhouette.** Stem-wide opening, apex-wide close — the word closes on itself, and the A's flat
  apex is a genuine, quiet event.
- **The footprint.** 331 × 100: the most compact wordmark the project has produced.
- **U3.** Untouched; still the best small asset the project has produced.

### Merely good
- **Distinctiveness.** Session 20 was right that it "looks normal, then doesn't." Most readers stop at
  normal: a plain geometric sans with a flat-topped A. **The identity's distinctiveness now rests on
  U3 more than on the wordmark** — the reverse of where it sat with Cut.
- **The wordmark–symbol relationship.** A near-match of angle and a shared shear. A resemblance.
- **The division grammar.** Typographic now. Works; does not distinguish.
- **The letterforms as drawn** — construction geometry, as the Session 20 sheet itself said. A type
  designer's optical pass is the first job of the next phase; **it must not touch the apex width, the
  stem or the tracking.**

### Unresolved
- **The A must stay Session 20's A** — flat apex of one stem width, with its crossbar. A literal
  rotation produces a lambda; recorded so that nobody later "purifies" the A in the name of the idea.
- **No axis and no intrinsic motion device.** Turned earns its layouts from the symbol and the
  typography. Normal; not what Sessions 20–23 were building toward; to be chosen knowing that.
- **Physical proof** still outstanding · **colour** untested by design.

# Recommendation

> ## Turned + U3 is ready to move into identity-system development, on two conditions.
> **(1)** The A is Session 20's A — flat apex of exactly one stem width, with its crossbar; a literal
> rotation is excluded as illegible.
> **(2)** The system's structure comes from U3 and typography, not from a line — the tail's devices
> (axis, pass, division-on-the-line) are retired with it and must not be reintroduced piecemeal.

It is a plain, compact, geometric wordmark with one quiet event in its last letter, and it costs the system
its line. The founders have chosen the trade; this board builds it properly. **No further logo
rounds.** Next: the fifteen identity-system steps, starting with final vector geometry and a type
designer's brief.

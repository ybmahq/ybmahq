# What changed

**Colour.** Amber removed; no replacement accent introduced. The system runs on **YBMA Indigo
#1F2C6B · Ink #151412 · Chalk #F5F4F0** (white for digital surfaces; Indigo 60 #3446A8 confined to
product UI states; Mist and Clay as quiet tints; warm greys). Roles defined precisely:

- **Ink** — the working colour: all text, the logo on light grounds, rules, icons, buttons; **also a
  ground** (the night surface: signage, avatars, workwear, dark product mode).
- **Indigo** — **two uses only:** a full field, or the one emphasised element on a chalk surface.
  Never a tint, never decorative, never more than once per surface. Indigo does not dominate because
  it is the primary; it dominates a surface or is absent from it.
- **Chalk** — the default ground (≥60%); type on indigo and ink.
- **Indigo and ink never meet** (contrast 1.5). Proportion on chalk: 64 · 20 · 10 · 6. Pages,
  slides and posts alternate chalk and indigo grounds — the turn, in colour.

**Emphasis without an accent — five devices, none a hue:** scale (the proof number is the largest
thing) · the field (one inverted panel per surface) · the curve (the composition's single round
element is the emphasised one; in charts the key bar is the only round-ended bar) · weight and the
stem (Halbfett vs Buch; a 2 px rule; underlined ink links; ink buttons) · photography (the hero
image is where colour enters — "colour comes from the world, not from the palette").

**Typography.** Söhne definitive: Buch (body) · Kräftig (labels, data, UI) · Halbfett (display,
headings) · Söhne Mono Buch (product). Söhne Breit Kräftig for a single cover word only. No second
family. Licensing documented separately (klim.co.nz; desktop seats, web tier, app; Helvetica Neue
stands in until installed). **Archivo withdrawn as a recommendation.**

**Re-tested:** architecture — all four divisions reversed on indigo, one rule, one colour, no
division colours · data visualisation, links, CTAs, charts, photography treatments (new: "the cut"),
signage (indigo field, ink door panel), workwear (indigo tee, ink jacket), slides (cover + proof
slide), social (indigo sentence / chalk number / photograph cadence), website hero (chalk; the
photograph is the colour), YBMA Technology header (chalk / indigo split) and dashboard (inverted
tile replaces the amber alert; semantic status colours defined in the UI kit, not the brand).

# Critical assessment

**Stronger without amber:** coherence · the mark is the most distinctive element on every surface ·
seriousness for finance and property · photography promoted to the source of colour · two-colour
print.

**Weaker:** the instant read of "attention" (a field or a big number takes longer than a bright) ·
sameness risk in feeds (the cadence must be kept) · execution dependence (a lazy application looks
like a consultancy — a real training cost).

**Distinctive?** Yes — the mark and the shape rule; restraint is the rarer look in the category.
**Young?** Yes, with a narrower margin — scale, tight Söhne, hard crops, real people, the round mark.
**Nigerian → African → global without colour symbolism?** Yes, more honestly than before; amber had
flirted with "gold". **Right for all four future sectors?** Yes.

**Genuine reason to keep an accent?** One, not a brand reason: product-interface states. Solved by
desaturated semantic UI colours in the YBMA Technology kit, confined to status indicators, explicitly
not brand colours. Every other job an accent did is solved by scale, field, curve, weight and
photography — and solved better, because none can be copied by adding an orange.

**Recommendation:** proceed with Indigo + Ink + Chalk; judge it on the revised applications and a
printed indigo swatch. If a surface feels flat, try a bigger number or an inverted field before
reconsidering colour.

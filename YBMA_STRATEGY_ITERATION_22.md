# What failed — three families, now closed

### 1 · Letter unions collapse back into a letter

Every attempt to fuse Y, B, M and A was taken over by whichever letter dominated.
**The eye finds a letter faster than it finds a union.**

| Sketch | Reads as |
|---|---|
| Notched stone *(block with a V cut into the top)* | **M** |
| Apex block *(solid A outline with its counter)* | **A** |
| Keystone with fork *(Y envelope with its notch)* | **Y** |
| Stacked envelopes *(Y's outline over A's outline)* | **X**, or an hourglass |

### 2 · A block sitting on the line reads as a laptop — **the key finding**

> **"Things sit on YBMA's line" works in the wordmark because letters are what sit on it. Replace the
> letters with a block and it becomes a screen on a keyboard, every time.**

Seam · Seat · Overhang · Lean · Angle union all read as **a laptop**. The Keystone variant read as
**a funnel**. A laptop is exactly the tech cliché the founders excluded in Session 1. **The whole
"block on a line" family is closed as a symbol.**

### 3 · The line cut as a white stroke reads as a letter again

Carving the YBMA line into a solid square was the right instinct. Drawn as a **white stroke on
black**, though, the stroke becomes the figure — and a bent stroke is **an L** (straight channel),
**a hockey stick** (angled channel) or **a tick** (internal channel).

# What survived — two stones

**The fix was to build the two pieces as solid masses rather than cut a stroke out of one.** The eye
then reads *two stones fitted into one whole*, and the gap between them reads as a **joint**, not a
letter.

### Every part of the joint comes from the wordmark

1. **The descent is on the Y's own angle** — the same ~29° as the Y's arms and the tail's terminal.
2. **The turn is square** — the same corner the tail makes when it leaves the Y.
3. **The run exits through the edge** — the same overrun as the tail running past the name.
4. **The end is sheared** — the lower stone's corner is cut exactly like the wordmark's tail
   terminal.

> **It is the tail's whole path, from the Y's arm to the sheared end, used to join two masses
> instead of underlining a name.**

### Why it answers "union"

Two separate masses made into one square, joined by the line YBMA draws under everything it owns.
For a company whose business is bringing separate businesses under one operator, that is the literal
structure — and **none of it needs a letter.** It also works without the story: a square of two
pieces with a describable cut.

---

# Three proportions

| | Name | Construction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| U1 | Balanced | Turn just below the middle; near-equal stones | **Out** — most stable, least specific. A split square |
| U2 | Wordmark ratio | Turn drops low; large upper mass over a band | **Close** — the square outline is still generic |
| **U3** | **Sheared** | **U2 with the lower stone's corner cut on the tail's terminal angle** | **RECOMMENDED** |

**Why U3:** in U1 and U2 all the distinctiveness is *inside* the square, and the inside is the first
thing lost at 16px. **U3 moves a brand detail — the sheared terminal — to the outline.** A clipped
corner survives any reduction, so at favicon size it is still this mark rather than a black square
with a nick in it.

**Specification (on a 120-unit square):** joint enters the top edge at x=12 · descends on the Y angle
(dx/dy = 0.5625) · turns square at y=86 · exits the right edge · **cut width 11** · lower stone's
right corner sheared on the same angle.

# Beside the wordmark — a division of labour

> **The wordmark is the identity. The symbol is where the wordmark cannot go.**

**Symbol:** favicon · app icon · social avatar · the endorsement on acquired companies
(*symbol · A YBMA COMPANY*) · embossing · signet · lanyard · document corner.
**Not:** as a lockup directly beside the full wordmark — the two would draw the same gesture twice.

---

# Against it — on record

- **The split-square genre is real.** Squares cut into pieces are common in architecture,
  engineering and consulting marks. The separation is the bent joint on YBMA's angle plus the
  sheared corner — **a real margin, not a wide one.**
- **A faint L survives in the joint.** Much weaker than the stroke versions, because the black masses
  dominate — but not zero. It reads as a symbol first.
- **Meaning is available, not obvious.** Nobody will see "the tail's path" unaided. It has to earn
  recognition through use, which is normal for a good symbol, but means early use does the work.
- **The sheared corner can read as a tag or label** if ever cut deeper than the wordmark's terminal.
  Angle and depth must stay locked to the tail.

---

# Recommendation

**Adopt U3 as the YBMA symbol. Retire the Y avatar.**

It meets both halves of the brief — **no letter**, and **a union**: two masses made one by the
identity's own line. It is also **the first small asset in the project drawn from the system rather
than cropped out of it.**

### Still open

- **Two proportions deserve one more look:** where the joint enters the top edge (the balance of the
  two stones) and the depth of the turn. Small moves on a shape that already works.
- **Carried from Session 21, unaffected:** overrun length · tail on the Y or the A.
  **If the tail moves to the A, this symbol must be mirrored to match.**

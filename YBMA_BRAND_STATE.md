## Project Overview

YBMA LTD is a company founded by four people — Yusuf, Benard, Michael and Assad — whose
initials form the name. It currently sells digital services (website development, software
development, IT consulting). The founders' long-term ambition is for YBMA to become a parent
company/group with divisions across technology, real estate, agriculture, financial services
and other sectors.

The purpose of this project is to build a brand identity that (a) wins business today as a
digital services firm, and (b) can carry a diversified group in 10–15 years without a rebrand.

Working method: multi-session, staged, strategy before visuals. Options and trade-offs are
presented; the founders decide. Recommendations are never recorded as decisions.

Status: **Iteration 22 complete. Founders rejected the Y avatar outright: no single letter, and

**Visual:** https://claude.ai/code/artifact/4f2ac2bf-702f-41c9-b0b0-a146e1919ae5
**Written:** `YBMA_STRATEGY_ITERATION_22.md`

> ### RECOMMENDED SYMBOL — U3 "SHEARED": **two solid stones fitted into one square**, joined along the
> tail's own path — **descending on the Y's arm angle, turning square, exiting the edge** — with the
> lower stone's corner **sheared exactly like the wordmark's tail terminal.** No letter anywhere.

**WHY IT IS A UNION:** two separate masses made one, joined by the line YBMA draws under everything
it owns — the literal structure of an operator bringing businesses under one roof. **It is the first
small asset in the project drawn *from* the system rather than cropped *out of* it.**

**THREE FAMILIES CLOSED, each for a reusable reason:**
1. **Letter unions collapse back into a letter** — the notched stone read **M**, the apex block **A**,
   the keystone **Y**, stacked envelopes **X**. The eye finds a letter faster than a union.
2. **A block sitting on the line reads as a LAPTOP — the key finding.** "Things sit on YBMA's line"
   works in the wordmark *only because letters are what sit on it.* Replace them with a block and it
   is a screen on a keyboard every time — the Session 1 tech cliché. **Closed as a symbol.**
3. **The line cut as a white stroke reads as a letter again** — an L, a hockey stick, a tick. **The
   fix: build two solid masses, so the gap reads as a joint rather than a figure.**

**WHY U3 OVER U1/U2:** the sheared corner moves a brand detail to the **outline**, so it survives
16px; in the plain-square versions all distinctiveness was inside the square and vanished first.

**Tests:** holds at 16px · **fits a circular crop with room to spare** (the Y never did) · works in
reverse · **joint narrows but stays open under ink gain.**

**Division of labour:** **the wordmark is the identity; the symbol is where the wordmark cannot go**
— favicon, app icon, social avatar, endorsement (*symbol · A YBMA COMPANY*), emboss, signet. **Never
as a lockup beside the full wordmark** (the same gesture twice).

**On record, not glossed:** the split-square genre is real — the margin is the bent joint on YBMA's
angle plus the sheared corner, *real but not wide* · a faint L survives in the joint · meaning is
available, not obvious, so recognition is earned through use.

**Still open:** where the joint enters the top edge, and the depth of the turn *(small moves)* ·
overrun length and Y-or-A carried from Session 21 — **if the tail moves to the A, the symbol must be
mirrored.**

### Method correction made this round

> The old notch-and-tab expressed *nothing created, nothing lost, something moved* by **removing**

> **The stronger executions do it in ONE operation: a single cut and a single move, with mass

**And a deliberate change of ingredient: the diagonal.** Every prior exploration was orthogonal,

| | Name | The rule | Verdict |

| **C2** | **THE FAULT** | *Every form is cut once, on the same 1:3 slope, off centre, and opened by one unit* | **RECOMMENDED AS A RULE** |

holds at 16px · excellent physical reproduction, since the gap is the detail and gaps reproduce
better than thin positives · **and it extends past the logo** — a photograph, a cover, a panel, a
hoarding, the gap between page sections can all be cut on the same slope and opened by the same
unit. **That is a grammar, and a grammar is what makes a simple mark recognisable through repetition
rather than through complication.**

## Commercial Decision — Session 8 `[DECIDED]`

### YBMA Technology sells: OPERATIONAL CLARITY

> **YBMA Technology builds the systems that let a business owner see and control what is actually
> happening in their business.**
>
> **Customer-facing:** *"We build the systems that show you what's actually happening in your
> business."*

**The reasoning.** *"Profit clarity"* is the best-evidenced option and **the wrong answer** — a
company whose proposition is *know your profit* cannot credibly sell a website, and would compete
with accountants and POS vendors on a narrow, low-margin promise. The customers' actual complaints
(*"it doesn't calculate automatically" · "reports are limited" · "nobody was entering information
properly"*) are not a reporting gap. **The information exists and does not flow. It is plumbing.**

Operational Clarity is the **union of C1 and C3** — the only direction carrying both confirmed
findings — and it positions against the real incumbent (C5) rather than against freelancers quoting
₦150,000.

**It carries every capability without strain:** websites *(the front door where enquiries enter,
captured rather than lost in WhatsApp)* · custom software · internal systems · integrations and
automation *(the mechanism)* · IT consulting *(as a piece of work with an output, never as advice —
C7)* · future products *(a productised reporting layer arrives naturally)*.

**BACKUP DIRECTION:** *technology that actually gets used* — positioned against the abandonment
pattern (C3). Weaker because it is a promise about YBMA's conduct rather than the client's outcome,
and unverifiable before purchase. **Use if Operational Clarity proves to be owned by accountants and
POS vendors.**

### YBMA Technology primary identity: **A BUSINESS SYSTEMS COMPANY**

Rejected: software/web development company *(commoditised; puts YBMA against ₦150k freelancers)* ·
business technology partner *("partner" appears nowhere in ten interviews; implies advisory value,
killed by C7)* · digital infrastructure company *(abstract and cold — C6)* · technology
consultancy *(C7)*.

**Why "systems":** it names what the customer is actually missing · covers every capability
without strain · keeps the focus on the business outcome · **is uncrowded in Nigeria** where
"software development company" is saturated · scales into products · and is plain enough to say to a
shop owner.

## Initial Customer Profile — Session 8 `[DECIDED]`

> ### Nigerian businesses running on partial systems.
> They have already bought technology. It answers some questions and not the ones that matter. Too
> big for a notebook, too small to have anybody whose job is the numbers.

**Why they become long-term clients:** each of the top four **needs more by its own nature** — more
locations, properties, SKUs, vehicles. The audit finds the ceiling; the systems work raises it; the
next location lowers it again. **Recurring by construction, not by retention effort.**

## Commercial Validation — Session 7 `[ALL HYPOTHESES, NOTHING CONFIRMED]`

### Objective

> **Find out whether a reachable Nigerian business will pay YBMA ₦500,000+ to tell it what it is

Not whether they like the idea; not whether they recognise the problem. **Whether money moves.**
Every conversation must end in an ask for a deposit.

### Target customer hypothesis `[HYPOTHESIS]`

Derived from the single respondent across ten interviews who behaved differently on price — the
mini-mart: 7 staff · 300–400 transactions/week · ~₦2m/week sales · **the only respondent paying
monthly for software (₦20–30k)** · highest prior tech spend (₦350k) · 4 staff on a shared system ·
**the only non-rejection at ₦500k** ("possible, if it is really good") · **the only urgency signal
in ten interviews** (chose ₦800k-in-3-weeks over ₦400k-in-3-months) · unmet need *"reports are
limited"* · a ₦70k loss never explained.

**Structural point:** its problem is **not** *"I have no system"* but *"I have a system and it won't
answer my question."* **The data already exists — which resolves input friction by construction.**

**MUST HAVE (all six):** pays for business software monthly (any amount) · already produces digital
transaction records · ≥150 transactions/week or ≥₦5m/month · staff other than the owner touch the
records · **has had at least one unexplained discrepancy** (the trigger in 2 of 2 actual purchases)
· owner is the decision-maker or will bring them.

**NICE TO HAVE:** bought software before and was disappointed · multi-location/vehicle · 7–10 staff
· expansion planned · has an accountant *(the deliberate OQ18 cell)* · Lagos/Abuja.

**EXCLUDE:** notebook-only, no digital records *(the offer cannot be delivered — tests a different
proposition)* · under ₦2m/month · **anyone personally close to a founder (a favour purchase is a
false positive and the most likely way this test gets corrupted)** · the five Round 1/2 respondents
· pre-revenue.

**Four challenges to the brief's own profile, on evidence:**
1. **"7–10 staff" is the wrong screen.** Headcount did not predict price tolerance — the 6-staff
   tailor refused ₦500k flat; the 3-staff transport operator engaged more. Record headcount, do not
   filter on it.
2. **"Lagos or Abuja preferred" is half a trap.** Both actual purchases were chosen on
   **recommendation**, and one specifically valued *"support people nearby."* A cold no in a city
   with no referral measures absence of trust, not absence of demand. **Run the majority where YBMA
   has referral access; keep 2–3 Lagos/Abuja as a contrast cell.**
3. **Deprioritise professional services.** Low transaction volume and a few large invoices — the
   mechanism has nothing to read. Prioritise POS retail, pharmacy, supermarket, hospitality with a
   booking system, logistics with trip records, clinics with billing, distributors.
4. **Do not exclude businesses with an accountant — recruit one deliberately.** OQ18 asks whether
   those who can pay already have this solved. Excluding them hides the answer most likely to kill
   the hypothesis.

**Cohort: 8** — 5 referred · 2 Lagos/Abuja · 1 with an accountant · **plus the mini-mart recorded
separately as warm/primed and excluded from the thresholds** (twice-interviewed, so a yes is weaker
evidence).

### Problem hypothesis `[HYPOTHESIS]`

> **They can see what they sold. They cannot see what they made. And every previous fix failed
> because it required somebody to enter data every day.**

`[EVIDENCE]` Margin blindness 5 of 5, independently, with quantified losses ₦25k–₦100k; 4 of 5
named profit not revenue as their one number. Input friction: 4 of 5 abandonments.
**What makes it non-generic:** two respondents already bought "know your numbers" and found it
*"limited."* They were not under-promised — they were **under-delivered**, because delivery required

### Proposition — layered, not chosen `[HYPOTHESIS]`

| Stage | Proposition |
|---|---|
| **Conversation opener** | *"Find out where the money is going."* — the live wound, and the actual trigger in both purchases. **Caution: can read as an audit where the suspect is family or long-serving staff. Opener only, never the proposal headline** |
| **The proposition** | *"The correct figures, from the records you already keep."* — outcome + mechanism |
| **The deliverable** | *"A weekly figure you can trust, without doing the work."* — concrete, priceable, demonstrable |

`[EVIDENCE]` The two respondents who preferred *"know what's actually happening"* over *"sort out

### Offer hypothesis — "The 90-Day Reconciliation" `[HYPOTHESIS]`

**Contains:** records intake (client sends **files, not labour**) · reconciliation of sales↔bank,
stock↔sales, purchases↔invoices · **a one-hour findings session in person with the
co-decision-maker** · the recurring weekly figure from the same sources with **no new data entry** ·

**Deliberately excludes:** building a new system *(new entry killed 4 of 5 previous attempts)* ·
replacing the POS · staff training · advice as a line item *(**zero of five have ever paid for
advice alone**)* · anything called transformation.

**Timeline 3–4 weeks** — the buying window in both real purchases was 2–4 weeks.

**Phase two (system build) exists but is NOT sold in the first conversation.** The engagement is a
**wedge**: it earns the right to the larger project, justified by numbers from the client's own data
rather than by a pitch. Mentioning it converts defined work into an open-ended commitment.

### Price hypothesis `[HYPOTHESIS]`

**One price per prospect. Standard: ₦650,000.** 50% deposit, 50% at findings.
**Why ₦650k first:** ₦500k caps what can be learned; ₦800k risks ending conversations before
information is produced. **The instrument is not the price — it is the negotiation. A number the
customer produces is revealed preference.**

| Response | Meaning | Next |
|---|---|---|
| Accepts without negotiating | At or below WTP | **Quote ₦800k to the next prospect** |
| Negotiates | **Their counter-number is the finding** | Record verbatim |
| Rejects on price, engages on value | Price wrong | **Reduced scope at ₦350–400k. Scope down, never discount** |
| Rejects on value | Problem or mechanism wrong | **Stop selling.** Price failure and value failure are different findings and must never be blended |

### Success thresholds — PRE-REGISTERED (n = 8, mini-mart excluded)

| | Condition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **STRONG** | **2+ pay a deposit at ₦500k+** | One sale can be an outlier. **Two independent purchases is the minimum evidence a segment exists rather than an enthusiast.** 25% close from cold outreach on a first-ever proposition is genuinely strong |
| **STRONG-MINUS** | 1 pays at ₦500k+ | Real but unrepeated — **run a second cohort of 8 before any positioning** |
| **MEDIUM** | 4+ recognise the problem sharply AND 2+ counter with a number / request reduced scope / ask different payment terms — **but nobody pays** | Problem real, offer/price/packaging wrong. **The most likely outcome, and actionable** |
| **WEAK** | Enthusiasm with **no** counter-number, no data access, no scheduled decision-maker meeting, no invoice request | **Nothing new — this is what the project has produced since Session 1 and must not be logged as progress** |
| **FAILURE** | **5+ do not recognise the problem as theirs, or say their POS / accountant already answers it** | **OQ18 answered negatively; the leading hypothesis dies.** Deliberately kept reachable |

**Scoring date must be fixed before fieldwork: three weeks after the last conversation. Unsigned
proposals are refusals on that date.** `[INTERPRETATION]` An unclosed proposal is a no with better
manners; without a fixed date the experiment never ends.

**Do not revise these thresholds once conversations begin.** They are recorded here so they cannot
be moved when early results disappoint.

### Decision matrix

| Result | Action |
|---|---|
| **2+ pay at ₦500k+** | Deliver flawlessly — the first engagement becomes the only case study that matters. Then a second cohort at **₦800k** to find the ceiling. **Only then** reopen positioning |

| **Everyone enthusiastic, nothing signed in 3 weeks** | **Score as WEAK, not pending** |

### Input-friction test — resolved without building a product

**The offer tests it by construction.** It reads records the business already produces; if the
client supplies files and one hour and a weekly figure then arrives with nobody entering anything,
the hypothesis is commercially tested with no product code.

**Feasibility order:** (1) export ingestion — POS CSV, gateway history, spreadsheet — **start
here, works today, no integration** · (2) spreadsheet ingestion · (3) bank statement parsing
(tedious, doable) · (4) POS API — **many Nigerian POS systems have no usable API; check per
prospect, never promise before checking** · (5) WhatsApp workflows — high value, **not in the first
offer** · (6) automated reporting — the core deliverable.

**AI is not the answer and must not be in the first offer.** `[INTERPRETATION]` The problem is
**plumbing, not intelligence.** Adding a model adds cost, unreliability and explanation burden to a
customer who has already said jargon means nothing. **If the first engagement needs AI to work, the
offer is wrong.**

**THE UNLOCK — and it changes OQ17:** `[INTERPRETATION]` **do the first engagement manually if
necessary.** If four founders can produce the weekly figure by hand from exports in two hours a
week, **the offer is deliverable today** and automation becomes a margin improvement, not a
prerequisite. **OQ17 blocks SCALING, not SELLING** — it must be answered before the tenth client,
not the first. **Sell first, automate second.** Nothing here requires the technical question
resolved before the first conversation.

### Master brand separation — the rule for this phase

> **A commercial win does not become the master brand.**

`[INTERPRETATION]` If the reconciliation offer sells, the temptation to make it the brand will be
immediate. **Resist it.** It would repeat the exact error of Sessions 2 and 3 — promoting whatever
currently looks strongest into the master-brand slot — and weld the parent company to a technology
service, the one outcome the founders have ruled out since Session 1.

**Belongs to YBMA Technology only:** margin visibility · input friction · records · reconciliation ·
the 90-Day Reconciliation offer · ₦650k · POS/WhatsApp/spreadsheet mechanics · re-buyer targeting ·
the 2–4 week trigger window.

**Belongs to the master brand:** *"knowing what things actually earn"* — a parent company's literal
function, and still the strongest master-brand candidate · no dependency / no traps as a group
value · the internal operating standard · branded-house architecture.

**What a commercial win actually licenses:** a validated technology-division proposition, a first
case study, real pricing evidence, and the credibility to reopen the master-brand question with
something other than inference. **That is a great deal. It is not a positioning.**

## Analysis Framework — PRE-REGISTERED (Session 5)

### Evidence classes — every claim must carry exactly one

**DIRECT BEHAVIOUR** *(they did it / it happened / observed — highest weight; R1's abandonment was
this class)* · **DIRECT STATEMENT** *(said plainly about their own situation, unprompted)* ·
**INDIRECT SIGNAL** *(implied, or stated only after prompting — record where in the interview)* ·
**INFERENCE** *(ours — labelled, and never included in any count)* · **UNKNOWN** *(not asked or not
answered — recorded, never silently dropped)*.

> **"Sounds strategically attractive" is not an evidence class.** Any claim without a classified
> evidence line is deleted.

### The structural rule

**Score the PROBLEMS, not the hypotheses.** Score each job/problem 0–3 on ten dimensions
(frequency · severity · current workaround · economic consequence · existing spending · willingness
to switch · willingness to pay · urgency · recognisability · differentiation potential), **then**
ask which hypothesis the highest-scoring problems support. Scoring hypotheses directly invites
fitting evidence to theory — which is how capacity survived three sessions.

Record **customer relevance and differentiation potential separately.** High relevance with low
differentiation is a coherent result (simplicity is the likely case), not a contradiction.

### Four bias controls

1. **Two-coder rule** — two founders code each transcript independently before discussing.
   Disagreements recorded, not resolved by seniority.
2. **Devil's advocate** — one founder assigned in advance to argue against the leading hypothesis.
3. **Quote-first write-up** — every finding leads with a verbatim quote or an observed behaviour.
   No quote, no finding.
4. **Record the absences** — maintain a *"not mentioned by anyone"* list. Round 1's best single
   finding (nobody mentioned a website) came from an absence.

### Required outputs after Round 2

Jobs-to-be-done record per respondent · problem scorecard · technology-experience comparison
matrix · alternatives table · language-test rankings · not-mentioned list · ELEVATE/HOLD/DROP per
hypothesis · recruitment log.

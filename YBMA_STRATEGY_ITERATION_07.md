# Commercial Validation Objective

> **Find out whether a reachable Nigerian business will pay YBMA ₦500,000 or more to tell it what
> it is actually making — using records the business already keeps.**

Not whether they like the idea. Not whether they recognise the problem. **Whether money moves.**

### What this test is deliberately not

- Not another interview round. The same five businesses have answered the price question twice and
  returned a consistent no. `[EVIDENCE]`
- Not a concept test. Concept tests measure enthusiasm, and enthusiasm is free.
- Not a survey with prices attached. Hypothetical pricing produced the ₦100k–₦300k range we already
  have; it cannot produce a purchase.

### The one thing that makes this test valid

**It must end in an ask for money — a deposit, an invoice, a signature.** Every softer endpoint
("they were very interested," "they asked for a proposal") is a result we already know how to
generate and which has told us nothing for six sessions.

# Target Customer Hypothesis

## Where the profile comes from

One respondent across ten interviews behaved differently on price. `[EVIDENCE]` The mini-mart:

| Attribute | Value |
|---|---|
| Staff | 7 |
| Transactions | **300–400 per week** |
| Weekly sales | **~₦2,000,000** |
| **Pays monthly for software** | **₦20,000–₦30,000 — the only respondent who pays anything** |
| Prior technology spend | **₦350,000 — the highest in the sample** |
| Staff using a shared system | **4** |
| Response at ₦500,000 | ***"Possible, if it is really good"*** — the only non-rejection |
| Ownership trade-off | Chose ₦600k-owned over ₦300k-controlled |
| Urgency trade-off | **Chose ₦800k-in-three-weeks over ₦400k-in-three-months — the only urgency signal in ten interviews** |
| Unmet need, their words | *"Some reports and deeper analysis"* · *"reports are limited"* |
| Unexplained loss | **₦70,000, cause never identified** |

**The critical structural point.** `[INTERPRETATION]` This business's problem is **not** *"I have no
system."* It is *"I have a system and it will not answer my question."* That distinction is the
whole basis of this experiment, because it means **the data already exists** — which resolves the
input-friction problem by construction rather than by product development.

## Challenges to the stated profile

The brief asked me to challenge its own assumptions. Four challenges, all consequential.

### Challenge 1 — "7–10 staff" is the wrong screen

`[EVIDENCE]` Headcount did not predict price tolerance. The tailoring business has **6 staff** and
refused ₦500k flat. The transport operator has **3** and considered it more seriously than the
tailor did. What predicted price tolerance was **existing software spend, transaction volume and
shared-system use.**

**Session 5 already established this principle — screen on money-at-risk, not headcount — and it
was right.** Keep headcount as a descriptor to be recorded, never as a filter.

### Challenge 2 — "Lagos or Abuja preferred" is half right and half a trap

Yes, test outside Kaduna. But `[EVIDENCE]` **both actual purchases in the study were chosen on
recommendation** — *"he was recommended by somebody I knew"* · *"they were recommended and had
support people nearby."* And the second buyer specifically valued *"support people nearby."*

`[INTERPRETATION]` **Cold-selling a brand-new proposition, from an unknown company, in a city where
YBMA has no referral and cannot show up in person, is the hardest possible version of this test —
and a "no" would be uninterpretable.** It would measure the absence of trust, not the absence of
demand.

**Recommendation: run the majority of the test where YBMA has referral access, and include two to
three Lagos or Abuja conversations as a contrast cell.** If the referred conversations convert and
the cold ones do not, that is a finding about go-to-market, not about the proposition.

### Challenge 3 — professional services should come off the priority list

`[INTERPRETATION]` The offer works by reading high-volume transaction records. A law firm, agency
or consultancy has **low transaction volume and a handful of large invoices** — they do not have
this species of margin blindness, and the mechanism has nothing to read.

**Deprioritise professional services**, despite its presence in the target definition. Prioritise
sectors that already generate digital transaction data: retail with POS, pharmacy, mini-mart,
supermarket, hospitality with a booking or POS system, logistics with trip and vehicle records, a
clinic with billing, a distributor with invoicing.

### Challenge 4 — do not exclude businesses that have an accountant. Recruit them deliberately

**OQ18 asks whether businesses that can pay already have this solved.** Excluding them would hide
the answer. **Include one or two with a bookkeeper or accountant as a deliberate test cell.** If
they say the accountant already answers it, that is the single finding most likely to kill the
leading hypothesis — and it is better to learn it in week one than after a positioning is chosen.

## The profile

### MUST HAVE — all six

| Criterion | Why it exists |
|---|---|
| **Already pays for business software monthly**, any amount | The strongest single predictor in ten interviews. The only respondent who pays is the only one who did not reject ₦500k |
| **Already produces digital transaction records** — POS, booking system, payment gateway, bank app exports | **The offer requires no new data entry. Without existing digital records it cannot be delivered, so testing such a business tests a different offer** |
| **≥150 transactions/week, or ≥₦5m/month turnover** | Below this, margin leakage is not material enough to justify ₦500k. The mini-mart runs 300–400/week on ₦2m/week |
| **Staff other than the owner touch the records** | This is what creates the reconciliation problem. The mini-mart has four; the solo operators do not have the problem in this form |
| **Has had at least one unexplained discrepancy in stock or money** | `[EVIDENCE]` **This is the trigger in 2 of 2 actual purchases.** Without it there is no urgency and no ROI story |
| **The owner is the decision-maker, or will bring them** | `[EVIDENCE]` 5 of 5 named a co-decision-maker — partner, spouse or family. A conversation without them cannot close |

### NICE TO HAVE

- **Has bought software before and been disappointed** — the re-buyer profile; prior purchase
  predicts price tolerance, ownership concern and literacy simultaneously
- Multi-location, multi-vehicle, or multi-shift
- 7–10 staff
- Expansion planned in the next twelve months
- An accountant or bookkeeper *(as the deliberate OQ18 cell — one or two only)*
- Lagos or Abuja *(two to three of the cohort)*

### EXCLUDE

| Exclude | Why |
|---|---|
| **Notebook-only businesses, no digital records** | The offer cannot be delivered without new data entry. Testing them tests a different proposition and will produce a misleading no |
| **Under ₦2m/month turnover** | ₦500k is implausible and the refusal tells us nothing we do not already know |
| **Anyone the founders are personally close to** | **A favour purchase is a false positive and it would poison the entire experiment.** This is the most likely way the test gets corrupted |
| **The five Round 1 / Round 2 respondents** | Primed by two interviews. See the note below on the mini-mart |
| Pre-revenue businesses and startups | No records, no volume, no money |

### The mini-mart — handle separately

It is the best-qualified prospect YBMA has and it has been interviewed twice, which means it is
primed. **Approach it, but record it as a warm/primed case outside the cold cohort**, and do not
count it toward the success thresholds. A yes from a twice-interviewed respondent is weaker
evidence than a yes from a stranger.

### Cohort

**Eight conversations**, plus the mini-mart separately.

| Cell | Count |
|---|---|
| Referred, YBMA's reachable market | **5** |
| Lagos or Abuja contrast cell | **2** |
| Has an accountant or bookkeeper *(the OQ18 cell)* | **1** |
| Mini-mart *(warm, counted separately)* | 1 |

Eight is achievable in three to four weeks across four founders, and it is enough to distinguish a
pattern from an enthusiast. **Keep a log of everyone screened and rejected, with the reason** — the
screen failures are data about market size.

# Problem Hypothesis

> **Businesses of this size can see what they sold. They cannot see what they made. And every
> existing attempt to fix that failed because it required somebody to enter data every day.**

`[EVIDENCE]` Both halves are supported.

**Margin blindness — 5 of 5, independently:**

> *"We thought we had more profit from accessories than we actually did… the margins were very
> small."* · *"You think you've made more money because you've sold plenty, then you realize the
> buying price has increased."* · *"The gross income looks good until you remove fuel and
> maintenance."* · *"Many orders but not enough cash."* · *"Some products weren't making as much
> profit as we thought."*

Four of five named **profit**, not revenue, as the one number they would want every morning. All
five quantified a specific loss: **₦45,000 · ₦60,000 · ₦80–100,000 · ₦25,000 · ₦70,000.**

**Input friction — 4 of 5 abandonments:**

> *"It looked like too much work to enter everything."* · *"Nobody was entering information
> properly."* · *"I wasn't using it consistently."* · *"It became troublesome."*

**What makes this hypothesis commercially interesting rather than generic.** `[INTERPRETATION]`
"Know your numbers" is the stock promise of every business-software vendor on earth, and two of
these respondents have already bought it and found it *"limited"* and *"not really what I
expected."* **They were not under-promised. They were under-delivered — because delivery required
data entry nobody sustained.**

So the defensible claim is not the outcome. It is **the outcome plus the mechanism**: the figures,
computed from records that already exist, with nobody entering anything new.

# Proposition Options

Four, developed from the evidence. The brief's three are included and improved.

---

## P1 — "The correct figures, from the records you already keep." `[RECOMMENDED — lead]`

**Customer problem.** They cannot see margin, and every previous fix demanded daily data entry.

**Customer outcome.** A figure they trust, arriving regularly, with no new work for anyone.

**Why they should care.** `[EVIDENCE]` 5 of 5 named a records task as the thing they would hand to
someone tomorrow. 5 of 5 said the notebook's single failure is that *"it doesn't calculate."*

**What makes it different.** The second clause. Every competitor promises the figures; **the
mechanism is the differentiator, and it is the reason the last attempt failed.**

**What would make it credible.** Doing it on *their* data in front of them. The records are already
digital, so the claim is verifiable within one working session — which is unusual for a services
promise at this price.

**What could make it fail.** If their existing POS reports turn out to be adequate once someone
actually reads them, YBMA's contribution collapses to interpretation, which is worth less. And the
phrase *"records you already keep"* invites *"so what do you need from me?"* — which must be
answered in one sentence.

---

## P2 — "Know what you're actually making — without your staff entering anything new."

**Problem.** Same, with input friction foregrounded.
**Outcome.** Margin truth at zero staff cost.
**Why care.** The transport operator's driver-management app died because *"nobody was entering
information properly."* This addresses that directly.
**Different.** Sharpest statement of the mechanism in the set.
**Credible.** Only by demonstration.
**Could fail.** Negatively framed — it defines itself against a competitor's weakness, which is a
weaker position than a positive claim. And *"anything new"* is a strong promise that a single
manual step would break.

---

## P3 — "Find out where the money is going." `[RECOMMENDED — conversation opener]`

**Problem.** The specific unexplained gap. `[EVIDENCE]` Every respondent had one, and every one
could name the figure.
**Outcome.** An answer to a question they have already asked and failed to resolve.
**Why care.** **This is the only proposition addressing a live wound rather than an improvement.**
It is also the actual trigger in both purchases: *"we couldn't immediately account for some
accessories, so I decided we needed something better."*
**Different.** It leads with loss, not information. Loss has urgency; information does not.
**Credible.** A 90-day reconciliation either finds the gap or does not. Falsifiable, which is rare.
**Could fail — and this is a real risk worth stating.** `[INTERPRETATION]` In a small Nigerian
business the suspect is often family or long-serving staff. Framing the engagement as *finding the
money* can read as an audit or an investigation, and an owner may decline for reasons that have
nothing to do with value. **Use it to open a conversation, not as the headline on a proposal.**

---

## P4 — "A weekly figure you can trust, without doing the work."

**Problem.** Same.
**Outcome.** A specific, concrete deliverable — closest to the *"one number every morning"* that
5 of 5 asked for.
**Why care.** It is the most tangible of the four. Nothing to imagine.
**Different.** It sells an artefact rather than a capability, which is easier to price and easier to
judge.
**Credible.** Highly — you can show a sample.
**Could fail.** It sounds small. A weekly report may not feel like ₦650,000 of value, even when the
work behind it is substantial.

---

# Recommended Test Proposition

**Layer them rather than choosing one.** Each does a different job, and the evidence supports each
in its place.

| Stage | Use | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Opening the conversation** | **P3** — *find out where the money is going* | Leads with the live wound. It is the actual purchase trigger |
| **The proposition** | **P1** — *the correct figures, from the records you already keep* | Outcome plus mechanism. The mechanism is the defensible part |
| **The deliverable** | **P4** — *a weekly figure you can trust, without doing the work* | Concrete, priceable, demonstrable |

`[EVIDENCE]` This ordering is supported by the language test. The two respondents who chose *"we
help you know what's actually happening in your business"* over *"we sort out your records"* were
**the two most delegated operations** — the transport operator with three drivers and the mini-mart
with seven staff. **The test cohort is entirely at that delegated end**, so the "knowing" register
is the right one for this segment even though "sorting out" won 3–2 overall.

**And what not to say, on the strongest evidence in the entire study:** *"digital transformation."*
**5 of 5 named it meaningless.** Nor *solutions*, *partner*, *platform*, *infrastructure*, or
*transformation* in any form. `[EVIDENCE]` None of those words appears in ten interviews except as a
complaint.

# Initial Offer

## The minimum credible offer

**Name it plainly.** Working name: **The 90-Day Reconciliation.** Not a product, not a platform — a
defined piece of work with a defined output.

### What it contains

| # | Component | What it actually is |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Records intake** | YBMA takes 90 days of whatever already exists — POS exports, bank statements, payment-gateway history, supplier invoices, the Excel sheet, the stock book photographed. **The client supplies files, not labour** |
| **2** | **Reconciliation** | Sales against bank, stock against sales, purchases against supplier invoices. Find the gaps and quantify them |
| **3** | **The findings session** | One hour, in person, with the owner and their co-decision-maker. **What you actually made, by product line / vehicle / service — and where the money went.** This is the moment the engagement is judged |
| **4** | **The recurring figure** | Set up so the same numbers arrive **weekly** from the same sources, with **no new data entry by the client or their staff** |
| **5** | **Handover** | Files, logic, spreadsheets, accounts, credentials — in the client's name, from day one. Written into the contract |
| **6** | **Support window** | **60 days**, stated with a date, priced in |

### What it deliberately does not contain

| Excluded | Why |
|---|---|
| **Building a new system** | `[EVIDENCE]` A new system means new data entry, which is what killed 4 of 5 previous attempts |
| **Replacing their POS** | They paid for it, four staff use it, and replacing it is the most expensive way to start |
| **Staff training on new software** | Training is input friction wearing a friendly name |
| **Strategy, consulting, advice as a line item** | `[EVIDENCE]` *"Have you ever paid anyone just for advice? No."* — both buyers. **Zero of five have ever paid for thinking alone** |
| **Anything called transformation** | 5 of 5 |

### Timeline: three to four weeks

`[EVIDENCE]` The buying window from trigger to payment in both actual purchases was **two to four
weeks.** A three-month engagement does not fit the urgency that creates the sale. And 4 of 5 chose
the slower cheaper option in the trade-off — **except the mini-mart, the one prospect in our target
profile, which chose faster and more expensive.** Speed matters to this segment specifically.

## Why this is the right minimum offer

1. **It is deliverable now.** No product, no platform, no integration required. `[BLUEPRINT CHECK]`
2. **It requires no new data entry by construction** — which is the entire input-friction
   hypothesis, tested by delivering rather than by building.
3. **It has a computable return.** Against a ₦70,000 unexplained loss that recurs, plus margin
   misallocation across a ₦2m weekly turnover, ₦650,000 is arguable with arithmetic rather than
   adjectives.
4. **It produces a recurring relationship** — the weekly figure — which is the recurring revenue the
   group ambition needs and which Session 3 identified as YBMA's structural gap.
5. **It proves or disproves the hypothesis in one engagement.**

## And the path it opens — do not sell this yet

If the reconciliation reveals a structural problem — the POS cannot track what it needs to, purchase
prices are not captured, staff process is broken — **then** a system build is justified. And it is
justified **by numbers YBMA produced from the client's own data**, not by a pitch.

`[INTERPRETATION]` This is the commercially important part: **the ₦500–800k engagement is a wedge,
not the whole business.** It earns the right to the larger project that YBMA originally wanted to
sell cold. **Do not mention phase two in the first conversation** — it converts a defined piece of
work into an open-ended commitment, which is exactly what makes buyers at this level anxious.

# Price Test

## The design

**One price per prospect. Standard price: ₦650,000.**

### Why ₦650,000 first

- Anchoring at **₦500,000** caps what can be learned — you can never discover that someone would
  have paid ₦800,000.
- Anchoring at **₦800,000** risks ending conversations before any information is produced.
- **₦650,000 leaves room in both directions, and the counter-offer becomes the measurement.**

`[INTERPRETATION]` The instrument here is not the price. **It is the negotiation.** A number the
customer produces is revealed preference; a number they react to is not.

### The ladder is adaptive, not presented

| What happens | What it means | What to do next |
|---|---|---|
| **Accepts ₦650,000 without negotiating** | Price is at or below willingness to pay | **Quote ₦800,000 to the next prospect** |
| **Negotiates** | **The number they counter with is the finding** | Record it verbatim. Hold the price once, then decide |
| **Rejects on price but engages on value** | Value understood, price wrong | Offer **reduced scope at ₦350,000–₦400,000** — 60 days instead of 90, one line of business instead of all. **Scope down, never discount** |
| **Rejects on value** | The problem or the mechanism is wrong | **Stop selling.** Ask why. Record as a value failure, not a price failure. **These are completely different findings and must not be blended** |

### What is included at each level — decide before the first conversation

| | ₦400,000 reduced | **₦650,000 standard** | ₦800,000 extended |
|---|---|---|---|
| Records reconciled | 60 days, one line | **90 days, all lines** | 90 days, all lines + supplier price history |
| Findings session | 45 min | **1 hour, on site** | 1 hour + a second review at 30 days |
| Recurring figure | Monthly | **Weekly** | Weekly + per-staff or per-vehicle breakdown |
| Support window | 30 days | **60 days** | 90 days |
| Handover | Full | **Full** | Full |

**Payment: 50% deposit to start, 50% on the findings session.** `[EVIDENCE]` 4 of 5 preferred a
one-off payment over monthly — *"I don't like unnecessary monthly commitments."* But the deposit is
non-negotiable, because **the deposit is the experiment.**

## Buying signals — real and false

`[INTERPRETATION]` Distinguishing these is the difference between a valid test and six more weeks
of optimism.

### Real signals

- Asks **who signs** or **how payment works**
- Asks for an **invoice** or a **contract**
- Asks **when you can start**
- **Counters with a number**
- **Gives you data access** — sends an export, opens the POS, forwards a statement
- **Asks a specific question about their own figures** *(the strongest of all — it means they have
  already started imagining the output)*
- Brings in the partner, spouse or accountant **on a scheduled date**
- Asks what happens after the 60 days

### False signals

- *"Very interesting"* · *"This is what we need"*
- **"Send me the proposal"** with no question about their own data — *the classic false positive*
- *"Let me discuss with my partner"* with no date attached
- Asks about YBMA's other clients but nothing about their own problem
- Compliments the idea
- Agrees with every diagnosis without adding one of their own

### What counts as a serious objection

An objection is serious when it is **specific and about them**: *"my POS already gives me that
report"* · *"my accountant does this every month"* · *"₦650k is more than I spent on the POS
itself"* · *"my staff will not send me the exports."*

An objection is not serious when it is **generic and about the world**: *"things are hard right
now"* · *"let me think about it."* `[INTERPRETATION]` The second kind is usually a polite no, and
recording it as an objection to be overcome is how founders talk themselves out of a clean result.

# Sales Conversation Script

**30–45 minutes. Nine stages.** Full carryable version in `YBMA_COMMERCIAL_VALIDATION_KIT.md`.

## The three rules that keep the test honest

**RULE 1 — The customer states a number before YBMA states a price.** Get them to quantify their
last unexplained loss, in their own words, before money is mentioned. Then ₦650,000 sits against a
figure they produced.

**RULE 2 — Do not describe the solution until they have described the problem.** And this is the
one that matters most:

> **If they do not describe a margin or reconciliation problem unprompted at Stage 3, stop selling.**
> Finish the conversation politely, learn what their real problem is, and **record a FAILURE data
> point.**

`[INTERPRETATION]` Educating a prospect into the problem is the single easiest way to corrupt this
experiment. It converts a clean negative into a false positive, and it is what four consecutive
sessions of discipline have been protecting against.

**RULE 3 — Ask for the deposit.** Every conversation ends with an ask. A conversation that ends in
agreement rather than an ask has produced nothing we do not already have.

## The stages

### 1 · Understand the business — 5 min
> *"Tell me what the business does and how a normal week runs."*
> *"How many people, and who touches the money and the records?"*
> *"Roughly how many transactions in a week?"*

### 2 · Understand current systems — 5 min
> *"What are you using now to keep track — show me if you can."*
> *"What do you pay for monthly?"*
> *"Who set it up? Can you change it yourself, or do you have to call someone?"*
> *"What does it tell you well? What does it not tell you?"*

### 3 · Identify the actual problem — 8 min · **THE DECISION POINT**
> *"Walk me through how you work out whether you made money last month — actually walk me through
> it."*
> *"How long does that take? Who does it?"*
> *"When was the last time a number surprised you?"*
> *"Has stock or money ever gone missing without you being able to explain it?"*

**If a margin or reconciliation problem does not emerge here, unprompted — stop. Rule 2 applies.**

### 4 · Quantify the consequence — 5 min · **CUSTOMER STATES THE NUMBER**
> *"How much was that gap, roughly?"*
> *"How often does something like that happen?"*
> *"Did you ever find out what caused it?"*
> *"If that's happening every month or two, what's that over a year?"*

**Write the annual figure down in front of them. Do not editorialise it. That number is the entire
price argument, and it is theirs, not YBMA's.**

### 5 · Show the proposed solution — 5 min
> *"Here's what I think is going on: you've got the information — it's in the POS, the bank, the
> invoices — but nothing is putting it together. So you can see what you sold and not what you
> made."*
> *"What we'd do is take ninety days of what you already have — exports, statements, invoices — and
> reconcile it. Then sit down with you and show you what you actually made, line by line, and where
> the gap is."*
> *"Then we set it up so those numbers reach you every week from the same records. **Nobody on your
> team enters anything new.**"*

### 6 · Explain what YBMA would do — 5 min
Scope, the six components, three to four weeks, what YBMA needs from them *(files, not labour)*,
and — unprompted — **ownership and the support window.**

> *"Everything is in your name from day one — the files, the logic, any accounts. And you've got
> sixty days of support after, with a date on it."*

`[EVIDENCE]` Both buyers volunteered that this is the question they wish they had been asked.
**Answering it before it is asked is free and it is the most differentiating thing YBMA can say.**

### 7 · Explain the investment — 3 min
> *"It's ₦650,000. Half to start, half at the findings session."*

**Then stop talking.** Do not justify, soften, or fill the silence. The response to the silence is
the data.

### 8 · Ask for the decision — 2 min
> *"Do you want to do it?"*
> If yes: *"I'll send the invoice today — can you look at it this week?"*
> If not yet: *"What would need to be true for this to be a yes?"*
> If they need someone: *"When can we sit with them? I'll hold the slot."*

### 9 · Observe the response — after
Score the conversation on the sheet in the kit within thirty minutes: signals present, objections
verbatim, their annual-loss figure, any counter-number, and one line on whether the problem was
raised unprompted at Stage 3.

# Proposal Structure

**Two pages. Sent within 24 hours.** Template in the kit.

| Section | Rule |
|---|---|
| **1. What you told me** | **Open with their words and their numbers, not YBMA's credentials.** Their quantified loss, their process, their unanswered question. Two or three sentences. **No "About Us." No methodology diagram** |
| **2. What it's costing** | Their annual figure from Stage 4. Theirs, attributed to them |
| **3. What we'd do** | The six components as plain statements. Under 150 words |
| **4. What you get** | The reconciliation findings · the weekly figure · the files |
| **5. Timeline** | Three to four weeks, with the findings session dated |
| **6. What we need from you** | Files and one hour. **Explicitly: no new data entry, no staff training, no new system** |
| **7. What's ours to do** | Everything else, named |
| **8. What you own** | Files, logic, accounts, credentials — in their name from day one. **A clause, not a promise** |
| **9. Support** | 60 days, with the actual end date written out |
| **10. Investment** | ₦650,000. 50% to start, 50% at findings. **One number, no options menu** |
| **11. Next step** | One action with a date: *"Confirm by [date] and we start [date]."* |

### What makes this not a generic agency proposal

No credentials section · no team photos · no methodology diagram · no phases with invented names ·
no tiered pricing table · **no mention of phase two.** `[EVIDENCE]` It opens with the client's own
loss figure and closes with a date. **Everything in it is either their information or a commitment
with a number attached.**

# Success Thresholds

**Pre-registered before the first conversation. Not to be revised afterwards.** `n = 8` cold and
referred conversations; the mini-mart excluded.

## STRONG SIGNAL

> **2 or more of 8 pay a deposit at ₦500,000 or above.**

**Why two.** One sale can be an outlier — an unusual business, a generous owner, a relationship that
was warmer than recorded. **Two independent purchases from unrelated prospects is the minimum
evidence that a segment exists rather than an enthusiast.** And a 25% close rate from cold outreach
by an unknown company on a first-ever proposition would be genuinely strong; most B2B services close
well below that.

**One deposit at ₦500k+ = STRONG-MINUS.** Real, and it requires a second cohort of eight before a
positioning is built on it.

## MEDIUM SIGNAL

> **4 or more of 8 recognise the problem sharply — and at least 2 either counter with a number,
> request reduced scope, or ask for a different payment structure — but nobody pays.**

**What it means.** The problem is real; the offer, price or packaging is wrong. **Actionable, and
the most likely outcome.** The counter-numbers become the price evidence six sessions have lacked.

## WEAK SIGNAL

> **Enthusiasm without any of: a counter-number, a data-access request, a scheduled meeting with a
> decision-maker, or an invoice request.**

**What it means.** Nothing new. This is the result the project has been producing since Session 1
and it must not be recorded as progress.

## FAILURE

> **5 or more of 8 do not recognise the problem as theirs, or say their existing POS, accountant or
> bookkeeper already answers it.**

**What it means.** **OQ18 answered in the negative, and the leading hypothesis dies.** This is the
outcome the design is most careful to leave available, because it is the one that would save YBMA
the most money.

## Why these thresholds are appropriate

1. **The endpoints are money, not sentiment.** Deposits and counter-numbers, never interest.
2. **They distinguish a price failure from a value failure.** Those demand opposite responses and
   blending them is how companies discount their way into a market that was never there.
3. **Failure is genuinely reachable.** A threshold no realistic outcome could trigger is decoration.
4. **The medium band is the most likely outcome and it is made useful** rather than being treated as
   a near-miss.
5. **Two, not one, for strong** — because the entire project has been on guard against building
   strategy on n=1, and it would be inconsistent to abandon that at the point where real money is
   involved.

# Input-Friction Test

> **Can YBMA credibly reduce the administrative work required to maintain useful business
> information — without building a complete product first?**

## The answer, and it unlocks the whole thing

**Yes — because the offer as designed tests it by construction.**

The engagement reads records the business **already produces**. If the client supplies files and one
hour, and a weekly figure then arrives with nobody entering anything new, **the input-friction
hypothesis has been tested commercially without a line of product code.**

## Approaches, ranked by feasibility for a four-person team

| # | Approach | Feasibility | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Export ingestion** — POS CSV, payment-gateway history, spreadsheet | **Highest.** Works today, no integration, no vendor permission | **Start here. This is the offer** |
| **2** | **Spreadsheet ingestion** | Highest — 4 of 5 already keep Excel | Include |
| 3 | **Bank statement parsing** (CSV or PDF) | Medium — PDF parsing is tedious but well-understood | Include; budget time for it |
| 4 | **POS API integration** | **Varies by vendor, and many Nigerian POS systems have no usable API** | Check per prospect during screening. Never promise it before checking |
| 5 | **WhatsApp workflows** | Medium-low — Business API involves cost, approval and template constraints | High value later. **Not in the first offer** |
| 6 | **Automated calculation and reporting** | High — this is ordinary work | The core deliverable |

## Two things to say plainly

**AI is not the answer here, and should not be in the first offer.** The brief was right to flag
it. `[INTERPRETATION]` The problem is **plumbing, not intelligence** — reading exports, matching
transactions, computing margin. Adding a model adds cost, unreliability and explanation burden to a
customer who has already told us that jargon means nothing. **If the first engagement needs AI to
work, the offer is wrong.**

**And the unlock:** `[INTERPRETATION]` **do the first engagement manually if necessary.** If four
founders can produce the weekly figure by hand from exports in two hours a week, **the offer is
deliverable today** and automation becomes a margin improvement rather than a prerequisite.

**That reframes OQ17 significantly.** The capability question does not block selling — it blocks
**scaling.** YBMA needs to answer it before the tenth client, not before the first. **Sell first,
automate second.** Nothing in this design requires the technical question to be resolved before the
first conversation, which removes the last reason to delay. `[BLUEPRINT CHECK]`

# Master Brand vs YBMA Technology

**Explicit, and held deliberately apart.**

## Belongs to YBMA Technology only

Margin visibility · input friction · business records · reconciliation · operations · the 90-Day
Reconciliation offer · the ₦650,000 price point · POS, WhatsApp and spreadsheet mechanics ·
re-buyer targeting · the 2–4 week trigger window.

**None of this may be promoted to the master brand.** `[INTERPRETATION]` "We reconcile your stock
against your bank statements" is an excellent thing for a technology division to say and a
meaningless thing for a company that will one day own farmland.

## Belongs to the YBMA master brand

**"Knowing what things actually earn"** — determining the real economics of each holding and
allocating accordingly **is a parent company's literal function.** This is the one idea that
survives the stretch to real estate, agriculture, financial services and future acquisitions, and
it remains the strongest master-brand candidate from Session 6.

Plus: no dependency and no traps as a group value · the internal operating standard · branded-house
architecture with division naming and endorsed acquisitions.

## The rule for this phase

> **A commercial win does not become the master brand.**

`[INTERPRETATION]` If the reconciliation offer sells, the temptation will be immediate and strong:
make it the brand. **Resist it.** It would repeat the exact error of Sessions 2 and 3 — promoting
whatever currently looks strongest into the master-brand slot — and it would weld the parent company
to a technology service, which is the single outcome the founders have said from Session 1 they must
avoid.

**What a commercial win would actually license** is a validated technology-division proposition, a
first case study, real pricing evidence, and the credibility to reopen the master-brand question
with something other than inference. That is a great deal. It is not a positioning.

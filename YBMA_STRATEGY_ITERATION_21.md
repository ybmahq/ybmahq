# STUDY 1 — THE AVATAR

### The diagnosis that shaped the study

> **The tail is a relational form.** In the wordmark its power comes from running *under and past
> the whole name* — **it needs something to run under.** Cropped to the Y alone the line has no
> object, the gesture loses its reason, and what survives is decoration.
> **That, not size, is why the current avatar feels thinner than the wordmark.**

### Four options drawn

| | Option | What it is | Five-second read |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Crop** *(current)* | The Y with its tail, ending inside the frame with a margin | **A stylised Y** |
| **B1** | **Bleed** | Same elements, recomposed — the line runs off the frame so the overrun survives the crop | **A fragment of something** |
| **B2** | **Plinth** | The line spans the full frame; the Y stands on it, descender merging into a base | **A letter on a bar** |
| **B3** | **Letter** *(control)* | The carved Y alone, no tail — testing whether the tail earns its place at this size | **A stylised Y** |

### Two symbol routes drawn and abandoned — with reasons, not padding

- **The Y–A pair** *(tail running from the first letter under the last — the name's bookends)*.
  Real logic, and **rejected on a strategic conflict: it creates a pronounceable fragment.** The
  state file is explicit that YBMA is **spelled, never spoken.** A speakable mark undermines the one
  fixed fact about the name.
- **An abstraction of the wordmark's diagram** *(mass above, line below, running right)*. Becomes a
  **text placeholder**, and multi-part layout diagrams have already failed here twice.

### VERDICT — B1, and it is a composition fix, not a new idea

**B1 is the best of the four** because it preserves the single quality that makes the gesture good —
**the line is still going somewhere** — by letting the frame do the cropping instead of stopping the
line short. **Adopt it.**

> ### Stated plainly, as the brief required
> **The avatar remains the weakest part of the system.** It reads as a stylised Y with a line, and
> no amount of composition changes that. **No symbol was found and none was manufactured.**

**The counter-argument worth weighing:** *YBMA may not need a symbol.* The mark's job at avatar size
is to be unmistakably YBMA's, and a carved Y with a line running off the edge does that, because
nobody else's Y looks like this. **A company that never speaks its name aloud is carried by its
letters. Forcing in a symbol to satisfy a category expectation is the move that produced four dead
rounds.**

# STUDY 2 — THE END OF THE TAIL

### The observation that makes this a real question

> **In the Cut alphabet every stroke terminal flares. The tail is the only stroke in the mark that
> ends without being shaped.** The square end is therefore not neutral — it is an inconsistency. It
> may be a deliberate one, since a rule is not a letter. **But it is a decision, and it had never
> been made.**

### Four endings

| | Ending | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | **Square** *(current)* | Honest, unambiguous — and slightly unfinished |
| **E2** | **Sheared** — cut on the **same angle as the Y's own arms** | **ADOPTED.** Authored rather than merely stopped |
| E3 | Flared — terminates like a letter | **Rejected.** A symmetrical flare at the end of a long horizontal is a **measurement terminator** — it turns the mark into a technical drawing of itself |
| E4 | Tapered — narrows over its final stretch | **Rejected.** Everything in Cut is monolinear; modulation at the end of a line is a **flourish**, however subtle |

### Why E2 wins

The cut takes the angle of the Y's arms, so **the tail ends on the geometry of the letter it came
out of.** A relationship, not a decoration — **nothing is added, one edge is rotated.** And it does
what the square end cannot: **it gives the line direction, which is what makes an overrun read as
*continuing* rather than as *stopping late*.**

**Magnitude: roughly nine units on a hundred-unit cap.** Small enough to disappear at stamp size —
**which is correct behaviour: visible where it can be seen, invisible where it could not have
survived anyway.**

# MOTION — intrinsic, not invented

**One behaviour drawn; everything else refused.**

> ## The line writes the name.
> It leaves the Y, travels right, and **each letter arrives as the line passes beneath it.**

**Nothing was added to the identity to make this work.** The recorded idea is already *"YBMA lays a
line and things sit on it"* — so **the motion is the static mark performed, not an effect applied to
it.**

**And it settles the endpoint question from a second, independent direction:** a travelling line
needs a **leading edge.** The sheared terminal *is* a leading edge — so the static mark reads as a
frame taken from the movement. **The static form is stronger because the motion is imaginable.**

**Refused:** bouncing · easing past and returning · thickening · glowing · cursor behaviour · drawing
twice. Each makes it a sports brand or a software product. **One pass, one direction, no return.**

# CREATIVE JUDGEMENT — the six questions, answered plainly

**Is the current avatar best?** **Nearly.** The elements are right; the composition was not. Let the
line run off the frame. **It still reads as a stylised Y, and that should be accepted rather than
solved by cleverness.**

**Is the current tail endpoint best?** **No.** It is the only unshaped terminal in a mark where every
other terminal is shaped. **The single genuine improvement this round produced.**

**Did any alternative genuinely improve the identity?** **One** — the sheared endpoint. The avatar
alternatives produced a better composition and no better idea.

**Does the animation potential strengthen the static form?** **Yes, and it is intrinsic rather than
invented.** It also independently confirms the sheared terminal.

**What should be kept?** Everything else, without qualification — carved flared letterforms, narrow
set, sharp apexes, Y-derived tail, overrun, asymmetric silhouette, and the avatar's elements.
**The direction was not the question this round.**

**What should be rejected?** The flared endpoint *(a dimension line)* · the tapered endpoint *(a
flourish)* · the plinth avatar *(a letter on a bar)* · the Y–A pair *(a speakable fragment of a name
that is never spoken)* · **every motion behaviour except a single left-to-right pass.**

# RECOMMENDATION

**Adopt D — and understand what D is: the current form with one edge rotated and one avatar
recomposed. Nothing about the direction changed, and nothing should have.**

- **Change, material:** the tail terminates on a **shear cut at the angle of the Y's arms.**
- **Change, minor:** the avatar lets the line **bleed off the frame.**
- **Hold:** letterforms · set width · apexes · flare · tail origin · overrun length · corner.
- **Record as a principle, not a system:** **one pass, left to right, no return.**

**Two of the four Session 20 drawing decisions are now closed** — the endpoint, and by implication
**the corner, which stays square**, because a square corner and a cut terminal read as one
deliberate pair. **Two remain open: the overrun length, and whether the tail belongs to the Y or the
A.** Neither was in this brief and neither was touched.

> **This round changed roughly nine units of a three-hundred-unit mark.** That is the correct outcome
> when the baseline is already good — and the studies were run properly enough that **keeping A
> would have been reported as a result rather than as a failure. One of the two studies said keep,
> and it is written up as keep.**

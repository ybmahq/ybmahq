# 2 · Creative Territory A — **RUNNING ORDER**

### 1 · Central creative idea

**YBMA is the hand on the controls.** The brand of a group whose actual competence is *keeping
things running* — and whose mark is the plate you find bolted to a working machine, saying what it
is and who stands behind it.

### 2 · One sentence

*A brand built from the language of things that are operated — controls, readouts, maker's plates —
because operating is what YBMA does.*

### 3 · Emotional effect

Capable. Alert. Unfussy. **In command without drama.** The specific reassurance of a well-run
operation — the feeling you get when you notice that something complicated is working and nobody is
panicking.

### 4 · Personality

Hands-on · exact · calm under load · quietly competent · **unshowy about difficult things.** Speaks
in readings, not adjectives.

### 5 · Visual world

Control surfaces. Switch plates and panel markings. Engraved labels. **Maker's and rating plates** —
the metal plates bolted to machines that state maker, model, capacity, date. Calibration ticks.
Indicator states. Fixing points and plate edges. Tabular figures.

**Physical controls throughout. Never screen UI.** The distinction between a switch plate and a
dashboard is the entire difference between this territory and a technology brand.

### 6 · Logo / mark implications

> **The monogram as a plate.**

Not a symbol applied to a surface — **a mark that behaves like an identification plate**: bounded,
stamped or engraved, stating what a thing is and who made it. A maker's plate is not decoration; it
is a claim of authorship and responsibility.

**Why this is structurally strong rather than merely clever:**

- **A plate is natively an endorsement stamp.** The hardest technical constraint in the brief — the
  mark must work as a primary lockup *and* as a small *"a YBMA company"* mark — is solved by the
  concept itself rather than by a workaround.
- **A plate is recognisable without the name.** The silhouette and the plate logic carry even when
  the letters are too small to read.
- **It quietly carries the ownership value.** A plate stays with the object. Permanent, transferable,
  and it says *this was made by us and it is yours.*
- **It works on every sector:** a plate on software, on a building, on equipment, on a document.

### 7 · Typography direction

Engineered rather than styled. **The register of machine plates and industrial labelling** — high
legibility at small sizes, slightly narrow, unornamented but with real mechanical character.
Crucially **not Swiss-neutral**, which is where the institutional-minimalism failure mode lives.
**Tabular and monospaced figures matter enormously** — this is a brand that states readings.

### 8 · Colour philosophy

**Functional colour: colour that means something rather than colour that decorates.** The logic of
indicator states — live, standby, attention — applied to a group's divisions.

One dominant industrial neutral *(metal-adjacent, warm rather than cool)* plus one or two signal
colours that carry meaning. **Explicitly not:** blue technology, black-and-gold luxury, the
ochre-terracotta "African palette," or a fintech gradient.

### 9 · Graphic language

Panel divisions. Engraved rules. Calibration ticks and scales. Indicator states. Plate edges and
fixing points. Data set in tabular columns as a *design element* rather than a chart.
**Division identity by functional coding**, the way a control panel distinguishes circuits.

### 10 · Photography / image direction

**Real operations, unstyled.** Hands on things. Stock rooms, vehicles, workshops, switchboards,
loading bays, the inside of a working business. Available light. Close. **Unglamorous by policy.**

**People at work — never portraits.** No arms-folded founder shots, no boardrooms, no white
backgrounds. This is where the territory's warmth comes from, and it is not optional.

### 11 · Motion direction

**Mechanical.** Things engage, switch, settle into position with weight and detent. Nothing floats,
nothing fades, nothing bounces. A switch either throws or it does not. Readouts increment; they do
not animate decoratively.

### 12 · Subtle African identity

**This is the territory's quiet strength, and it is embedded rather than pasted.**

`[INTERPRETATION]` **Keeping things running is a genuine Nigerian competence.** Generators,
inverters, boreholes, the roadside workshop, the person who keeps the thing working when the grid
does not. *Operator* has real local texture — it is not an imported metaphor.

Material references available without a single motif: **enamel signage, painted metal, galvanised
surfaces, workshop paint, hand-stencilled plant markings.** These are Nigerian industrial vernacular
— specific, unmistakable, and invisible to anyone hunting for clichés.

### 13 · YBMA Technology inside the system

It fits most naturally of the four divisions — a systems business *is* control surfaces. **That is a
risk, not a benefit** (see the attack). The division must be held to the same industrial register as
the others rather than being allowed to pull the system toward software aesthetics.

### 14 · Properties / Agriculture / Financial Services inside the system

**Properties:** a plate on a completed building, stating who built it and when. Genuinely used in
construction, and it reads as accountability rather than branding.
**Agriculture:** plates on equipment, stores and vehicles; batch and lot marks; scales.
**Financial Services:** the instrumentation register carries gravity easily — readings, rates,
states. **The strongest of the three territories on financial services.**

### 15 · What makes it distinctive

**Maker's-plate logic for a holding company is uncommon**, it solves the endorsement problem
natively, and it is recognisable without the wordmark. It also sidesteps every banned cliché — no
globe, arrow, building, lightbulb, Y-symbol, gradient, tech blue or African pattern.

### 16 · What could go wrong

| Risk | Severity |
|---|---|
| **Reads as a dashboard / SaaS brand** — the exact thing to avoid | **Primary.** Mitigated only by insisting on *physical* controls and *operational* photography, never UI |
| **Drifts vintage or heritage** — brass plaques, Victorian machinery — which would violate *youthful* | **High.** The reference must be **contemporary machine tools and current equipment**, not antiques |
| Too industrial, too masculine, too cold | Real. The photography is the counterweight and must be warm and human |
| Precision tips into the institutional minimalism already rejected | Moderate. Guard with mechanical character in the type, not neutrality |

### 17 · Adjacent references

Contemporary machine-tool and equipment identification · aviation and marine panel design ·
Japanese industrial equipment branding · enamel workshop and plant signage · calibration and
metrology instruments. **Deliberately not** the over-cited mid-century product-design canon, which
would pull this straight into institutional minimalism.

### 18 · Why it fits YBMA specifically

It is the only territory where **the mark means the strategy.** A maker's plate *is* the operator
claim: we made this, we run this, our name is on it, and it stays with you. It carries the
accountability idea the project has circled since Session 1 — without making it a slogan, and
without depending on four named founders.

# 3 · Creative Territory B — **THE BENCHMARK**

### 1 · Central creative idea

**YBMA establishes the reference point.** Before anything is built, a surveyor sets a datum — a
fixed mark everything else is measured from. YBMA is what other things get measured against.

### 2 · One sentence

*A brand built from the apparatus of establishing where things actually stand — datum marks, level
lines, measured proportion — because knowing the real position is the discipline underneath
everything YBMA does.*

### 3 · Emotional effect

Exact. Grounded. Quietly authoritative. **Permanent.** Confidence expressed as precision rather
than volume.

### 4 · Personality

Measured · rigorous · patient · truthful about numbers · **unbothered by having to prove itself.**

### 5 · Visual world

Survey and datum marks. Level lines. Setting-out marks and chalk lines. Benchmark plates cut into
stone. Dimension notation. Rebar grids and block modules. Coordinate systems. Measured proportion
throughout.

**The apparatus of establishing truth before construction** — not finished buildings, which is the
banned cliché.

### 6 · Logo / mark implications

A monogram derived from a **datum or setting-out mark** — the small, exact marks cut or painted to
establish level and position. Potentially a mark that is simultaneously a letterform and a reference
point. Bounded, precise, excellent at small size.

Weaker than A as an endorsement stamp — a datum mark reads as a *position*, not as *authorship*.

### 7 · Typography direction

**Architectural — drawn rather than styled.** Proportional discipline, generous display spacing,
tabular figures. A **stencil influence** is available from site setting-out and plant marking, which
is Nigerian construction vernacular without being a motif.

### 8 · Colour philosophy

Derived from building materials **as found and heavily abstracted** — the specific grey-green of
local cement, chalk white, oxide, the blue of a surveyor's chalk line. Low chroma, one exact accent.

> **Explicit warning: this palette's gravitational pull is straight into the terracotta-and-ochre
> "African earth tones" cliché.** That is now a global design default and it must be actively
> designed against, not merely avoided in intention.

### 9 · Graphic language

**Measure.** Datum lines, dimension marks, modular grids, coordinate references. **Division identity
by position in a system rather than by colour** — an unusually elegant idea for a group, and the
territory's best single device.

### 10 · Photography / image direction

Structures being set out and land being measured. Wide, exact, human-scale. **Before-states rather
than finished glamour** — the site before the building, the field before the crop.

### 11 · Motion direction

**Precise placement.** Elements align, level, and lock to grid. Measured. Nothing elastic.

### 12 · Subtle African identity

Nigerian construction as a living visual culture — setting-out marks, block modules, hand-lettered
site notices, the particular colour of local cement. And a deeper, genuinely local claim:
`[INTERPRETATION]` **in a market where measurement is frequently approximate, being the fixed
reference point means something specific.**

### 13 · YBMA Technology inside the system

Fits well. A systems business measures and reports. It does not dominate, which is an advantage
over A.

### 14 · Properties / Agriculture / Financial Services inside the system

**Properties: perfectly — and that is the problem.** Too perfectly.
**Agriculture:** land measurement, setting out, yield. Natural.
**Financial Services:** benchmarks, rates, standards. Works, with gravity.

### 15 · What makes it distinctive

The datum idea is fresh, specific and ownable, and *division-by-position* is a genuinely original
system device.

### 16 · What could go wrong

| Risk | Severity |
|---|---|
| **Becomes a property or construction brand** the moment YBMA Properties exists | **Primary — the mirror of A's technology risk, and harder to escape** |
| **Fails the energy requirement.** Static, humourless, low velocity — *youthful, energetic, experimental* are all difficult here | **High. This is the territory's real weakness against the brief** |
| Slides into the institutional minimalism already rejected in Session 3 | **High.** Precision plus restraint is exactly that failure mode |
| The palette drifts to African-earth-tone cliché | Moderate, and named above |

### 17 · Adjacent references

Surveying and metrology instruments · architectural drawing conventions · Japanese construction and
engineering identity · Swiss-influenced systems design **with grit added deliberately.**

### 18 · Why it fits YBMA specifically

It is the most direct visual translation of *knowing the real number* — the confirmed customer
finding — and it is the most naturally permanent-feeling of the three, which suits a group that
intends to hold things for decades.

# 4 · Creative Territory C — **MARKET RATE**

### 1 · Central creative idea

**YBMA carries the energy of Nigerian commerce itself** — dense, fast, numerate, resourceful — built
from how business actually looks and sounds where YBMA comes from, and executed to a global
standard.

### 2 · One sentence

*A brand built from Nigerian commercial vernacular — price boards, hand-painted signage, tallies,
stacked density — disciplined into something that holds its own anywhere.*

### 3 · Emotional effect

**Alive.** Confident, quick, warm, a little bold. The feeling of a place where a great deal is
happening and somebody is unmistakably on top of it.

### 4 · Personality

Energetic · streetwise · numerate · warm · **unapologetic about where it comes from.**

### 5 · Visual world

Layered information. Hand-painted commercial signage. Price boards. Tally and count marks. Stacked
goods. Dense typographic hierarchy. High-contrast colour used with real confidence. **Contemporary
African modernism** — the graphic assurance of West African commercial vernacular, disciplined
rather than imitated.

### 6 · Logo / mark implications

**A wordmark with genuine character** rather than a neutral monogram — drawn letterforms with weight
and personality, **the kind that survives being painted on a wall by hand.** YBMA as a name that is
said aloud and painted, not merely set in a typeface.

Weakest of the three as an endorsement stamp, and most dependent on the wordmark for recognition.

### 7 · Typography direction

**The most expressive of the three.** A display face with real character — condensed, weighted,
confident — paired with a plain workhorse. **Numerals carry disproportionate weight:** prices,
tallies, figures, rates.

### 8 · Colour philosophy

**The most colour of the three, used with discipline.** Not a muted palette — high chroma,
confidently combined. **Drawn from commercial paint and enamel, not from "African earth tones."**
A specific green, a specific red-orange, a strong blue that is emphatically not corporate
technology blue.

### 9 · Graphic language

Layered panels. Tally and count systems. Price-board hierarchy. Bold rules. **Density as a
principle** — controlled, never chaotic.

### 10 · Photography / image direction

**People and commerce.** Markets, shops, yards, vehicles, transactions, hands exchanging things.
Warm, close, alive, real. **The strongest human content of the three by a wide margin.**

### 11 · Motion direction

Quick, decisive, rhythmic. Things stack, count, total. Velocity with control.

### 12 · Subtle African identity

**The most African of the three, and the most dangerous.** It is embedded in *commercial*
vernacular rather than traditional motifs — which is the right instinct and avoids the pattern trap
entirely. But it sits one step from the *generic African* outcome the brief rules out, and the
distance is entirely in the execution.

### 13 · YBMA Technology inside the system

**Fits the technology division's actual customer better than A or B do.** The register is domestic,
commercial and plain — which is exactly what ten interviews said this customer responds to.

### 14 · Properties / Agriculture / Financial Services inside the system

**Agriculture:** excellent — produce, commerce, movement.
**Properties:** difficult. A dense, energetic brand on a property development reads downmarket.
**Financial Services: very difficult.** Financial services requires gravity, and this territory's
entire value is velocity. **This is the structural weakness.**

### 15 · What makes it distinctive

**The most distinctive and most memorable of the three by a distance**, and the only one that fully
delivers *youthful, energetic, experimental* without qualification.

### 16 · What could go wrong

| Risk | Severity |
|---|---|
| **Cannot credibly support financial services** | **Primary, and structural rather than executional** |
| **Reads downmarket internationally** — a problem for a brand whose path is Nigeria → Africa → Global | **High** |
| Tips into the *generic African* cliché the brief bans | **High.** The distance is all in execution |
| **Maximalism punishes weak execution.** This territory requires the most skill to land | High |
| Most likely of the three to age | Moderate |
| **Most copyable.** Another African company could adopt this register largely unchanged | High |

### 17 · Adjacent references

West African commercial and hand-painted signage · contemporary African design studios ·
Nigerian and Ghanaian sign-painting traditions · and in global terms the confident-graphic register
of certain Latin American consumer brands.

### 18 · Why it fits YBMA specifically

It is the only territory that would make a young Nigerian engineer *want* to work there, and the
only one that feels like it was made by the people who made it. It answers the brief's emotional
requirements more completely than either alternative.

### The two symmetrical failures

# 6 · Recommended Territory

## **A — RUNNING ORDER**

### Why it wins

**1 · The mark means the strategy, not just the mood.** A maker's plate states authorship,
responsibility and permanence. That *is* The Operator — we made this, we run this, our name is on
it, and it stays with you. Neither alternative achieves that: B translates the discipline, C
translates the feeling.

**2 · It solves the hardest technical constraint by concept rather than workaround.** The mark must
work as a primary lockup **and** as a small one-colour *"a YBMA company"* endorsement. **A plate is
natively both.** That constraint has been on record since Session 2 and this is the first idea that
does not have to fight it.

**3 · It is the strongest of the three on financial services and the only one strong on all four
sectors.** Instrumentation carries gravity; density does not. For a group that intends to reach
financial services, C is disqualified and B arrives via property.

**4 · It is recognisable without the wordmark** — which matters disproportionately for a spelled
four-letter initialism nobody can yet pronounce on sight.

**5 · Its Africa is embedded, not applied.** *Keeping things running* is genuine Nigerian
competence — generators, workshops, the person who keeps it working when the grid does not.
Available materially through enamel, painted metal and stencilled markings. No motif, no pattern,
no explanation required.

**6 · It avoids both identified failure modes** without needing to be talked out of them.

**7 · It carries the energy requirement adequately, and the deficit is fixable.** Operating is
active. Where it needs warmth, the photography supplies it — and unstyled photographs of real work
are both warmer and more distinctive than styled portraits.

### Nothing is wasted — the other two become layers

**A genuine hierarchy, not a blend:**

| Layer | From | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **The world** | **A · Running Order** | Controls, plates, readouts, states. The identity |
| **The craft standard** | **B · The Benchmark** | Measure, proportion, exactness, tabular figures. **The discipline A's execution is held to** — and it keeps A from being merely industrial-flavoured |
| **The humanity** | **C · Market Rate** | Warm, close, real photography of people at work. **The specific antidote to A's coldness risk**, and where the Nigerian dimension becomes visible |

That layering is defensible because each contributes a different *kind* of thing — a visual world, a
craft standard, and an image policy. It is not three aesthetics averaged.

### Why the other two lose

**B · The Benchmark loses on two counts, one fatal.** It fails the energy requirement — *youthful,
energetic, experimental* are all difficult in a world built from datum marks — and more seriously,
**it becomes a property brand the moment YBMA Properties exists, and that cannot be designed
around.** It also sits directly in the institutional-minimalism failure mode already identified as
my own Session 3 error. **It loses on structure, not taste.**

**C · Market Rate loses on one count, and it is structural.** **It cannot credibly support financial
services** — the territory's entire value is velocity and financial services requires gravity. It
also reads downmarket internationally on a Nigeria → Africa → Global path, it is the most copyable
of the three, and it demands the most execution skill to avoid the *generic African* outcome the
brief bans. **It is the best brand of the three for YBMA Technology and the wrong brand for YBMA.**
`[INTERPRETATION]` That is precisely the trap the project has been avoiding for nine sessions: the
division's natural register colonising the parent.

### Challenging the recommendation, as instructed

**Is the maker's plate a platform or a clever device?** The honest test is whether it generates a
full system. It does: engraved type, panel divisions, calibration marks, indicator states, fixing
points, division plates, tabular data as ornament. And it has one strong recognisable object, which
most identity systems lack.

**The real risk is period drift.** Maker's plates can read *brass and Victorian*, which would violate
*youthful* outright. **The reference set must be contemporary machine tools and current equipment,
never antiques.** This is the single biggest execution risk and it is now on record.

**And the one thing that would overturn it:** if the founders' answer to the operator question is
*capital-led* — appoint managers and hold — then *hands on the controls* is a false claim and
**B becomes the recommendation.**

### The five design rules this platform imposes

1. **No screen UI in the brand's own visual language.** Physical controls only. This is what keeps
   YBMA from becoming a technology brand.
2. **Contemporary equipment references, never antique.** Period drift is the primary execution risk.
3. **Every graphic element must state something true.** If it only looks good, it is ornament and it
   does not belong.
4. **The photography carries the warmth.** If the images are styled, the territory fails.
5. **The plate must work at endorsement scale before it is approved at lockup scale.** Design the
   small use first — it is the constraint that has governed this project since Session 2.

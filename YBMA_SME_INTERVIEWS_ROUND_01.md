# SME Interview Responses

## 1. Phone & Gadget Shop — Kaduna

### 1. Business context

**Question:**  
> Tell me a little about your business and what you're trying to achieve over the next 1–3 years.

**Answer:**  
We sell phones, accessories, chargers, earbuds, smart watches, things like that. We also do screen replacement and some repairs. For the next few years, I want to grow the business and probably have another branch, but the main thing is to have enough customers and also be able to properly track the business.

### 2. Current constraint

**Question:**  
> What's the biggest thing stopping your business from doing more right now?

**Answer:**  
Honestly, keeping track of everything. Stock is probably the biggest issue. Sometimes you have plenty of some things and don't even realize another item is finished until somebody asks for it.

### 3. Technology

**Question:**  
> Does technology play any role in that?

**Answer:**  
Yes, definitely. We use WhatsApp a lot. Customers send what they want there. We also advertise on Instagram sometimes. For stock, we mostly use a notebook and sometimes Excel.

### 4. Previous experience

**Question:**  
> Have you ever paid someone to build a website, software, app, POS system, e-commerce platform, or anything similar for your business?

**Answer:**  
Yes. We once paid someone to set up a small system for us. It was supposed to help with stock and sales.

**Question:**  
> Tell me about that experience.

**Answer:**  
The problem was that the person built it and left. If something wasn't working, we had to call him. Sometimes he was busy or travelling. Eventually we just stopped using it.

### 5. What went wrong

**Question:**  
> What was frustrating about it?

**Answer:**  
The fact that we couldn't really do anything ourselves. Even small issues meant calling the developer.

**Question:**  
> What did you wish they'd done differently?

**Answer:**  
They should have trained us properly and given us access to everything. We shouldn't have to depend on one person just to use something we paid for.

### 6. What mattered when choosing

**Question:**  
> When you were choosing who to work with, what made you choose them?

**Answer:**  
We mostly looked at price and whether the person seemed like he knew what he was doing. At that time, we didn't really know what questions to ask.

**Question:**  
> What made you reject the others?

**Answer:**  
Some were too expensive. Some also made it sound too complicated for what we needed.

### 7. Outcome

**Question:**  
> After the project was finished, what did you actually get?

**Answer:**  
We got the system, but we didn't really get something we could manage ourselves.

**Question:**  
> And who held the keys?

**Answer:**  
The developer, basically.

**Question:**  
> Could you change it yourself?

**Answer:**  
No.

**Question:**  
> Did you have access to everything?

**Answer:**  
Not really. He had the hosting and everything.

**Question:**  
> Were you dependent on the person who built it?

**Answer:**  
Yes. That's actually why we stopped using it.

### 8. Growth

**Question:**  
> Did what they built help you do more, or did you eventually outgrow it?

**Answer:**  
Initially it helped a little, but as the business grew, we needed something better. We have more products now and more transactions, so the old system wasn't really enough.

**Question:**  
> What happened?

**Answer:**  
We went back to our normal manual process because upgrading or changing the system meant going back to the developer.

### 9. Trust

**Question:**  
> What makes you trust a technology company with something important to your business?

**Answer:**  
Transparency. If somebody tells me exactly what they're doing, how much it will cost and gives me access to my own business information, I'll trust them more. Also, I want to know that if the person disappears tomorrow, my business won't disappear with him.

### 10. Final question

**Question:**  
> If you could change one thing about the way technology companies work with businesses like yours, what would it be?

**Answer:**  
I think technology companies should make things simpler for business owners. Don't build something that only the developer understands. And don't make the business owner dependent on you for every little thing.

# 3. Ride-Hailing / Transport Operator — Kaduna

### 1. Business context

**Question:**  
> Tell me a little about your business and what you're trying to achieve over the next 1–3 years.

**Answer:**  
I do transport. I use the apps sometimes, but I also have private customers that call me directly. My goal is to have more regular customers and eventually have more cars instead of just driving one car myself.

### 2. Current constraint

**Question:**  
> What's the biggest thing stopping your business from doing more right now?

**Answer:**  
Getting consistent customers and managing the money. Some days are good, some days are not. Fuel and maintenance also take a lot.

### 3. Technology

**Question:**  
> Does technology play any role in that?

**Answer:**  
Technology is basically part of the business. I use the ride-hailing apps, WhatsApp, Google Maps, bank apps, everything.

### 4. Previous experience

**Question:**  
> Have you ever paid someone to build a website, software, app, POS system, e-commerce platform, or anything similar for your business?

**Answer:**  
I haven't paid anyone to build anything specifically for me. I mostly use existing apps.

**Question:**  
> Tell me about that experience.

**Answer:**  
The existing apps are useful for getting customers, but you don't really control the whole experience. You basically operate according to how the platform works.

### 5. What went wrong

**Question:**  
> What was frustrating about it?

**Answer:**  
Sometimes you don't really control anything. They decide how things work and you just use it. If there's a problem with the platform, there's not much you can do.

**Question:**  
> What did you wish they'd done differently?

**Answer:**  
I'd like more control over my customers and my business rather than everything being tied to the platform.

### 6. What mattered when choosing

**Question:**  
> When you were choosing who to work with, what made you choose them?

**Answer:**  
For me, it has to be reliable and easy to use. If something is complicated, I won't use it when I'm driving.

**Question:**  
> What made you reject the others?

**Answer:**  
If it's difficult to use or I don't see enough benefit from it, there's no point. Also, if there aren't enough customers using it, I won't bother.

### 7. Outcome

**Question:**  
> After the project was finished, what did you actually get?

**Answer:**  
The apps help me get customers, but the customer relationship is not completely mine. Sometimes you meet a customer through the platform and then everything is still controlled by the platform.

**Question:**  
> And who held the keys?

**Answer:**  
The platform.

**Question:**  
> Could you change it yourself?

**Answer:**  
No.

**Question:**  
> Did you have access to everything?

**Answer:**  
No.

**Question:**  
> Were you dependent on the person who built it?

**Answer:**  
Yes, because if the platform has a problem, there's nothing I can do.

### 8. Growth

**Question:**  
> Did what they built help you do more, or did you eventually outgrow it?

**Answer:**  
The apps help with getting work, but if I want to build my own transport business with multiple cars, I'll need something where I can manage my own drivers and customers.

**Question:**  
> What happened?

**Answer:**  
For now I'm still using the platforms, but I'm thinking more about having my own customer base and eventually managing other drivers.

### 9. Trust

**Question:**  
> What makes you trust a technology company with something important to your business?

**Answer:**  
Reliability. If I'm putting my business inside your system, I need to know the system won't just stop working. And I need proper support when there's a problem.

### 10. Final question

**Question:**  
> If you could change one thing about the way technology companies work with businesses like yours, what would it be?

**Answer:**  
Technology companies should understand that small businesses don't have IT departments. If something goes wrong, I'm the one dealing with it, so it has to be simple.

# 5. Mini-Mart / General Retail — Kaduna

### 1. Business context

**Question:**  
> Tell me a little about your business and what you're trying to achieve over the next 1–3 years.

**Answer:**  
We sell provisions, drinks, toiletries and other household items. It's a family business. We're trying to increase sales and hopefully open another location.

### 2. Current constraint

**Question:**  
> What's the biggest thing stopping your business from doing more right now?

**Answer:**  
Knowing exactly how much we're making. Sales are happening every day, but sometimes when you calculate everything at the end of the month, you realize the profit isn't what you thought.

### 3. Technology

**Question:**  
> Does technology play any role in that?

**Answer:**  
Yes. We use POS for payments and WhatsApp for some customers. We also have a computer, but we don't really have one system where everything is together.

### 4. Previous experience

**Question:**  
> Have you ever paid someone to build a website, software, app, POS system, e-commerce platform, or anything similar for your business?

**Answer:**  
Yes, we've used POS software before.

**Question:**  
> Tell me about that experience.

**Answer:**  
It helped with sales and knowing some of the stock, but we still had to keep manual records.

### 5. What went wrong

**Question:**  
> What was frustrating about it?

**Answer:**  
The main problem was support. When there was an issue, you had to wait for somebody to come and fix it. Sometimes the software would also not match what we were doing physically.

**Question:**  
> What did you wish they'd done differently?

**Answer:**  
I wish they made it easier for us to solve small problems ourselves and gave us better support when something serious happened.

### 6. What mattered when choosing

**Question:**  
> When you were choosing who to work with, what made you choose them?

**Answer:**  
Price, recommendation and whether other businesses were already using it. If another shop owner tells me they've been using it successfully, that's more convincing than somebody coming to advertise to me.

**Question:**  
> What made you reject the others?

**Answer:**  
Some were too expensive and some didn't have enough people around us using them. I didn't want to be the first person testing something.

### 7. Outcome

**Question:**  
> After the project was finished, what did you actually get?

**Answer:**  
It helped with sales and some stock management, but we still had to keep manual records.

**Question:**  
> And who held the keys?

**Answer:**  
The company that provided the software.

**Question:**  
> Could you change it yourself?

**Answer:**  
Only some things. Anything serious, we had to contact them.

**Question:**  
> Did you have access to everything?

**Answer:**  
No, not everything.

**Question:**  
> Were you dependent on the person who built it?

**Answer:**  
Yes, for anything beyond the basic things we could do ourselves.

### 8. Growth

**Question:**  
> Did what they built help you do more, or did you eventually outgrow it?

**Answer:**  
We eventually needed more features, especially reporting and stock management. When you're dealing with more products, you need to know which ones are moving and which ones are just sitting there.

### 9. Trust

**Question:**  
> What makes you trust a technology company with something important to your business?

**Answer:**  
For me, recommendation is very important. I want to know other businesses are using it successfully. Then support. If something goes wrong, somebody should actually answer.

### 10. Final question

**Question:**  
> If you could change one thing about the way technology companies work with businesses like yours, what would it be?

**Answer:**  
Technology companies should not just collect money and disappear. If you're building something for a business, you should continue to support the business.

# What changed in the method

The old notch-and-tab said *nothing created, nothing lost, something moved* by **removing** and then
**adding** — two operations. That is why it drifted toward keys and batteries.

> **The stronger executions do it in ONE operation: a single cut and a single move, with the mass
> conserved by construction rather than by inspection.**

**And one deliberate change of ingredient.** Every previous exploration was orthogonal — squares,
bars, right angles — and that is a large part of why it kept arriving at institutional or
industrial. **Two candidates introduce a diagonal.** Not for fashion: a diagonal is the cheapest
source of energy available, and energy is what the earlier work over-corrected away from.

# The six mechanisms

| | Name | Rule | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **THE SLIP** | *The displacement equals the displaced.* A part of width *w* moves by exactly *w* | **Survives.** Best self-referential rule; **very quiet as a form** |
| **C2** | **THE FAULT** | *Every form is cut once, on the same 1:3 slope, off centre, and opened by one unit* | **RECOMMENDED — as a rule** |
| **C3** | **THE SWING** | *Elements rotate about a point on their own edge, never their centre* | **Killed.** Reads as a page turn or an arrow; acute angles reproduce badly |
| **C4** | **THE OVERRUN** | *One element always exceeds the frame, by one unit* | **Killed twice over.** Reads as a bar chart — **and it is not displacement.** Nothing moved; something was extended |
| **C5** | **THE CARRY** | *Each element carries forward, displaced by a constant* | **Killed.** Ascending offset bars read as a growth chart |
| **C6** | **THE EXCHANGE** | *What YBMA takes out of one place is exactly what appears in another — the void and the solid are congruent* | **Right idea, wrong form.** Two separated elements are a diagram, not a mark. **The rule survives the execution** |

# Recommendation

> ## C2 · THE FAULT — as a rule, not yet as a finished mark

**The rule clears the bar.** *Cut once, on a fixed slope, off centre, and open by one unit.*

- **Conserves mass by construction**, not by inspection — a geological fault adds and removes
  nothing
- **Checkable and reproducible** at any scale
- **The diagonal supplies the energy** the earlier work lacked, while both masses stay orthogonal —
  so it moves without becoming a slash
- **Best small-size behaviour of the six.** Two bold masses, one interval; holds at 16px
- **Excellent physical reproduction** — the gap is the detail, and gaps reproduce better than thin
  positives
- **And it extends past the logo.** A photograph, a cover, a panel, a hoarding, the gap between
  sections on a page — all can be cut on the same slope and opened by the same unit. **That is a
  grammar, and a grammar is what makes a simple mark recognisable through repetition rather than
  through complication.**

## The honest qualification

**The mark does not yet clear the bar.** A square split by a diagonal gap is a known device, and the
only things separating this from generic are the specific slope and the off-centre cut. **That is a
fine margin, and I am not going to claim it is resolved when it is not.**

## Attacking my own recommendation

**1 · It may be too quiet.** Two masses and a gap is close to the minimum a mark can be.
Distinctiveness would come almost entirely from the grammar and from repetition — which works, but
slowly, and YBMA has no recognition to spend.

**2 · The split-square association is real.** Diagonal divisions are common. I cannot promise the
slope alone carries it.

**3 · The base form is still a square.** **Every exploration in this project has started from a
square. That may be the unexamined assumption** — and it is the most likely source of the sameness
the founders keep reacting to.

# What the failures teach

**Bars are permanently out.** C4 and C5 both died on the chart read — the third time bars have
failed this project, after T3. **Horizontal and stacked bars should not be proposed again for this
brand.**

**C3** died on the page-turn read *and* on physical reproduction — acute angles are poor for
embroidery, casting and engraving.

**C6** died as a form while its rule survived. *"The void and the solid are congruent"* is the best
sentence produced in this round and it deserves a better carrier than two orphaned elements.

---

# The question the next round must answer

One question, and nobody has asked it yet:

> **Does the mark have to be a bounded geometric mass at all?**

Every candidate across three sessions has been a solid shape sitting in a square. Displacement could
equally be expressed through **an interval in a continuous system**, through **type itself**, or
through **a form with no outer boundary.**

**Until that is tested, C2 is the strongest thing available rather than the strongest thing
possible.**

# The lever that was never pulled

Three faults were on record:

1. **The Y crossing bisected the M's counter** — settled, stays out.
2. **Without an event it read as a 1970s conglomerate** — that diagnosis blamed the *symmetry*.
3. **Four letters can't be read at 16px** — true of any four-letter block. Manage, don't solve.

> **Fault 2 was misdiagnosed. It was probably the weight, not the symmetry.**

Every version of this block used a stem of 10 units on a 40 cap — **25%, very heavy.**
**Ultra-bold caps inside a filled square is what a 1970s conglomerate literally is.**

---

# Six settings tested

| | Setting | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **Baseline** — heavy stem, filled block, 6-unit datum | The version as it stood. The problem |
| **B** | **Lighter** — stem 10 → 7, **one change only** | **Counters open, the block breathes, the conglomerate weight drops away** |
| **C** | **No container** — positive letters, no filled square | Much lighter, stops being a stamp. **Which is also why it fails** |
| **D** | **Hairline datum** — band 6 → 2 | Doesn't fix the weight; disappears small |
| **E** | **Airy** — lighter letters + more internal margin | **Most contemporary of the six** |
| **F** | **Displaced corner** — a corner of the block moved to the opposite corner | **T4's move, applied to the block** |

---

# Recommendation — **B + F**

## Lighter letters, displaced corner

**The weight was the problem, not the symmetry.** Dropping the stem from 10 to 7 removes the 1970s
reading **on its own** — without touching a letterform, without adding a device. **That is the
cheapest fix in this entire project and it was never tried.**

**The displaced corner solves what the datum never could.** It gives the block a *specific
silhouette*, so at 16px — where four letters are unreadable regardless — there is still a
recognisable shape. Every other setting leaves a generic square at that size.

**And it is T4's move applied to the block.** A corner taken from one side, appearing at the other.
Same size, same shape, nothing created, nothing lost. **That means the two directions you have
responded to are the same idea rather than competing ones** — the block carries the name, the
displacement carries the identity.

---

# The interesting reject

**C · No container** is genuinely the most contemporary thing in the set — positive letters, no
filled square, datum as a rule. **But it cannot be a stamp, and the endorsement system needs a
stamp.**

**Keep it as a secondary treatment** — large-format, headers, signage — where the container isn't
doing work.

**D · Hairline datum** — a thinner line doesn't address the weight and it vanishes at small size.
**A alone** remains the baseline problem.

# What I designed from

Not shapes. Four facts:

1. **The name is spelled, never spoken as a word** — Why-Bee-Em-Ay. A call sign, not a word.
2. **Structurally, Y and A are open letters; B and M are closed.** The name opens at both ends and
   is solid in the middle. **A real property of these four letters and nobody else's.**
3. **YBMA will hold unrelated businesses** — the identity must mean nothing sector-specific.
4. **It is at the start of its story.** The brief's best line governs: *a company going somewhere,
   not one pretending to have arrived.*

# Verdict

## None of these clears the bar either.

Three survive as **ideas** — T1's open/closed structure, T4's punctuation logic, T7's weight
gradient. **None is a finished identity, and I am not nominating one to keep the process moving.**

---

# The pattern across three resets — the actual finding

> **Every round has explored symbol and wordmark at the same time, and so has done neither
> properly.**

Look at what survived: **T1 and T7 are wordmark ideas with no small-size asset. T4 is a small-size
asset with no presence.** Each survivor is missing the same thing in the same place.

**That is not coincidence.** It is what happens when *"is YBMA symbol-led or wordmark-led?"* is left
open round after round. Every exploration splits its depth across both and reaches neither.

**That decision is yours, it takes one conversation, and it would double the depth of the next
round** — by letting it go all the way down one path instead of halfway down two.

---

# Recommended next steps, in order

**1 · Decide the type of identity.** Symbol-led · wordmark-led · or wordmark-plus-small-mark.
**This is the decision that has been missing for five sessions.**

**2 · If wordmark-led** — T1's open/closed structure is the strongest starting point in this set,
with T4's punctuation as the companion small mark. Together they cover the gap neither covers alone.

**3 · And to say it plainly.** A wordmark of this kind is won or lost **in the drawing** — at a
level of craft that needs a type designer iterating by eye, at size, on paper.

**The strategy, the rules, the tests and the exclusions are done and they are good.** Eleven
sessions of business thinking are genuinely valuable and will brief a designer well. **The remaining
work is hand work, and commissioning it is likely to get you further than another round here.**

That is not a refusal to continue — if you want more territories I will produce them. It is the
honest assessment of where the remaining risk sits.

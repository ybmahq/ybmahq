# 1 · Diagnosis — the concern is correct

Not a matter of taste. The fault is measurable.

**The stem bisects the M's counter.** A 10-unit stem dropped into a 20-unit opening splits the M's
single V into two narrow notches — so the eye reads a **distorted M, or a W**, rather than an M with
something passing through it.

**And it terminates at y=71 — exactly the height where the M's inner diagonals begin.** It touches
nothing. It reads as a stalactite: attached to nothing, stopping for no reason.

> **The deeper problem is structural, not cosmetic. The M's centre-top is empty *by definition* —
> that is what an M is. Anything descending into it will float. No adjustment of length or width
> fixes this, because the void is the letter.**

This also means the Session 11 claim that the crossing was the "load-bearing construction decision"
was **rhetorical rather than visual.** It was load-bearing for the *concept*. It was damaging the
*form*.

---

# 2 · The M, redrawn

A separate fix, applied to every construction. Nothing in it was changed to accommodate the Y.

| | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| V apex | 57% down — bottom-heavy, cavernous opening | **43%** |
| Wedge at centre | 8 units — starved against 10-unit stems | **10 units, equal to the stems** |
| Termination | Pinched point | **Flat, 6 units** — answers the A's flat apex and the B's chamfers |

**The M now holds up viewed alone, which is the test that matters.**

# 3 · Six constructions tested

All use the redrawn M, so the only variable is the Y–M relationship.

| | Construction | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **A** | **CONTROL** — nothing crosses, unbroken datum | Best letterforms. **Inert.** A tidy symmetrical block with nothing to remember |
| **B** | **CROSSING RETAINED** — current construction | **Rejected.** A better M makes it *worse*: a confident letter now visibly has something jammed into it |
| **C** | **CROSSING SHORTENED** — stem stops at the M's cap line | Solves the stalactite. **The event shrinks to 6 units and is gone at 32px.** The control with extra steps |
| **D** | **BROKEN DATUM** — line breaks either side, drafting convention | **Rejected.** Conceptually literate, but the stem still enters the counter, and the 4-unit breaks vanish below 48px — leaving only the fault |
| **E** | **THE DROP** — no crossing; a vertical drops from the datum through the M/A gutter | **RECOMMENDED** |
| **F** | **STEPPED DATUM** — the level changes at the gutter | **The most interesting failure.** Conceptually excellent; uneven margins read as *misalignment* at small size. Keep for graphic language, not the mark |

---

# 4 · The finding that decides it

> **Every event at the scale of the datum's thickness disappears below 48px.**

C's shortened stem and D's line breaks are both gone at 32px, leaving the control with extra
complication attached.

**A distinctive event has to be bigger than the line it interacts with.** The crossing satisfied
that rule — which is why it was chosen — but it paid for it by damaging a letter.

**E satisfies the same rule in empty space.** A 63-unit vertical in the gutter is a far larger event
than a 14-unit stem, and it costs nothing.

---

# 5 · Recommendation — E · THE DROP, with the redrawn M

**The Y crossing is removed.** It was an implementation decision, not the strategy, and the geometry
shows it could not be made to work.

**The event moves to the gutter** — a vertical dropping from the datum, *below it only*, through the
empty channel between M and A to the block's edge.

- **Larger than the crossing**, so it survives to 16px
- **Asymmetric**, so the block never reads as a tidy grid
- **Touches no letterform at all** — the gutter has no ink in it
- **Runs below the datum only**, which is what stops it reading as a divider splitting the mark in half

**And it reads better as Running Order.** A line of record with a branch taken off it is
registration and distribution — closer to what an operating company does than a letter poking
through a rule ever was.

## What I am not claiming

**E is less conceptually elegant than the crossing was.** The crossing had the virtue that a
*letter* did the work; E's event is a graphic element in a gutter. That is a genuine loss and worth
naming rather than glossing.

But the crossing's elegance was rhetorical and its execution was broken — which is exactly the trap
the brief warned against. **A mark is judged on form. E produces four confident letters and one
deliberate event. B produces three confident letters and a damaged one.**

## If E is wrong, the fallback is A — not C or D

C and D are the control with invisible complications attached; neither earns its extra geometry.
**If the crossing must go and the drop is not wanted, take the plain block and let the wordmark and
colour carry the distinctiveness.** That is an honest option, not a failure.

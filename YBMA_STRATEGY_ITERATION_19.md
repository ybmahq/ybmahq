# What this round did differently

Three resets have produced the same failure in the same place. Session 17 diagnosed part of it:
every round explores a symbol and a wordmark at once and finishes neither. **There is a second
pattern underneath it, and it is more expensive.**

> **Every candidate in nineteen sessions has been judged by eye.** A mark was proposed, looked at,
> and killed for what it resembled — a key, a battery, a bar chart, a circuit trace, a maze, a
> 1970s conglomerate. Those reads were correct. But taste arrived *after* the drawing every time,
> and taste has now rejected eighteen rounds of drawings.

So this round added a test that does not depend on anyone's eye. Every finalist was built as drawn
geometry on an identical grid — **cap height 100, stem 15** — and put through the same four
conditions: a size ladder to 16px, a reverse field, and two levels of simulated **ink gain**,
modelling ink spread on absorbent stock, thread gauge in embroidery, draft angle in casting, and
the line weight a signwriter's brush actually lays down.

**The ink-gain test disqualifies the front-runner, confirms two candidates, and does it
arithmetically rather than by preference.**

---

# The consequence nobody had drawn from the name

`[INFERENCE]` A name that is **spelled rather than spoken** cannot be carried by a symbol early.
There is no sound to attach a shape to and no meaning for a shape to stand in for.

> **For the first ten years, YBMA is whatever its four letters look like.** A symbol placed beside
> them competes for the attention the letters still need.

This is the structural reason four rounds of symbol work produced nothing worth keeping. It is not
a taste finding, and it resolves **OQ-U** (symbol-led vs wordmark-led) on reasoning rather than
preference: **wordmark-led, with any small asset derived from the same construction rule.**

# The mechanical test — the finding of the session

Four finalists (C1, C2, C3, C9), identical conditions, identical dilation applied to all.

| Condition | C1 One V | C2 Trap | C3 Unclosed | C9 Control |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Primary 260px | holds | holds | holds | holds |
| Document 96px | holds | holds | softening | holds |
| Avatar 32px | holds | holds | **degraded** | holds |
| Favicon 16px | fails (even block) | fails (even block) | **fails (uneven)** | fails (even block) |
| Ink gain — stamp, print | bolder, intact | **improves — traps fill to correct shape** | **two-thirds of gaps closed** | bolder, intact |
| Ink gain — stitch, cast | bolder, intact | **intact** | **identity fully gone** | bolder, intact |
| Reverse | holds | holds | holds | holds |

> ## C3 UNCLOSED does not survive the last two rows.
> Its gaps close, and what remains is a wordmark with soft junctions and no idea in it. This is not
> a matter of degree — under stitch-and-cast conditions the identity is *entirely* gone.

**Why this matters beyond the drawing.** YBMA's recorded brand principle is *"built for the hardest
conditions, therefore good enough for the easiest."* **A logo that closes up when it is stitched
contradicts the brand it belongs to.** That is a structural cost, not an executional one.

### Stated fairly, on record

- The simulation is **uniform dilation**, a simplification. Real ink gain is directional and
  material-dependent, and a skilled embroiderer can hold a gap with the right stitch density and
  backing. **Session 18's instruction still stands: print it and stitch it.** But the direction of
  the result is not in doubt.
- **The 16px row does not discriminate.** *No* four-letter wordmark survives 16px, including the
  control. It cannot be used to argue that any candidate needs no small asset. What it shows is the
  *shape* of the failure: C1/C2/C9 collapse to an even block, C3 to an uneven one.

# RECOMMENDED · C1 — ONE V

> ### Three of the four letters in YBMA contain the same V. We drew it once and used it three
> ### times. The B is the only letter that doesn't have it, so it carries the weight.

**Construction:** cap 100 · stem 15 · one angle at 29.7° from vertical · the V identical in Y, M
and A · zero curves, including in the B · the A's apex flattened to exactly one stem width · every
corner of the B cut on the same slope.

### Why it fits

**It is found, not applied.** Every previous direction imposed an idea on the name — a
displacement, a block, a rule about closure. This one *reports a property the name already had.*
That is the difference between a concept the founders must be persuaded of and a fact they can
check in thirty seconds. And **nothing counts anything** — no founders, no sectors, no divisions —
so nothing expires when a founder leaves or a fifth business is bought.

### Why it is distinctive

The rule is specific to Y·B·M·A and **cannot be lifted onto another name.** It produces one
genuinely unusual letter — an all-straight B — and unusual letters are what people remember about
wordmarks. It is describable after one viewing: *"there are no curves in it, and the same V appears
three times."* **That sentence is the test C2 fails.**

### Why it scales and lasts

An alphabet, not a logo: it extends to YBMA TECHNOLOGY / PROPERTIES / AGRICULTURE without a
decision or a device, and **endorsement is the name itself in the same letters** — no badge, no
plate, nothing that has to survive sitting next to an acquired company's own brand. Straight-line
monolinear construction is **the most reproducible letterform there is** — stencil, brush, stitch,
mould, receipt printer. That is the African-origin argument made structural rather than decorative.

### Weaknesses — not glossed

1. **Genre proximity is real.** Constructed geometric alphabets are populated territory (Futura,
   Avant Garde and descendants). The splayed M and flat-apex A are where this could read as 1970s
   ITC pastiche. Defence is weight, fitting and restraint — not concept.
2. **No separate small mark, and the tests did not settle it.** Holds at 32px, fails at 16px exactly
   as the control does. **The weakest link in the recommendation.**
3. **Won or lost in the drawing.** What exists is construction geometry, not drawn letterforms.
   Optical correction — overshoot, joint thinning, real sidebearings — is the difference between
   this and a good version of this.
4. **The angular B could go wrong.** Too faceted and it is a computer font; too soft and the rule
   stops being visible. **The single riskiest decision in the direction.**

### The fallback, named in advance

**C2 THE TRAP** — weaker story, stronger nerve; it looks like a serious neutral wordmark until you
get close. The two are compatible: C2's junction cuts can be specified *inside* C1 as a production
detail. **An option, not a requirement** — adopting both risks a mark doing two things.

# Against the real world

| Candidate | Closest familiar category | Severity | What separates it |
|---|---|---|---|
| C1 One V | ITC-era constructed geometric type | Moderate | Moderate weight, open fitting. **The angular B has no equivalent in that genre** |
| C2 The Trap | 2016–21 "trappy" display type | Moderate | Traps sized to a stated reproduction spec, not to a look |
| C3 Unclosed | Open-counter display; tech/crypto wordmarks | Low | As a whole-identity rule, genuinely rare |
| C4 The Hold | **Crop marks; VC and consulting bracket marks** | **High** | Unequal arms and a fixed ratio — **honestly, not enough** |

**The category most successfully avoided is the one this project kept crashing into:** *African
conglomerate / generic holding company.* None of the four finalists reads that way, **because none
is a bounded geometric mass in a square.** That was the unexamined assumption Session 14 flagged and
never tested. It has now been tested by not doing it.

# Unresolved

- **The B — how angular?** Three settings should be drawn before anything else is decided.
- **The M's vertex depth.** Where the retro risk lives.
- **Is a separate small asset needed?** The tests did not answer it. If yes, it must come out of the
  same construction rule, not be invented beside it.
- **Traps in or out** — a production decision against a stated spec, not an eye decision.
- **OQ-A — operator or investor**, open since Session 8. Still does not block visual work.

# Next, in order

1. **Decide between C1 and C2** — or name the element of C1 that is wrong (the B, the M, the A's
   apex, the weight), so the next round moves a lever instead of restarting.
2. **Draw the four letters properly.** Optical correction, not construction. **The step never taken
   in nineteen sessions.**
3. **Extend to a working set** — the glyphs needed for TECHNOLOGY, PROPERTIES, AGRICULTURE,
   FINANCIAL SERVICES and A YBMA COMPANY. About twenty, not an alphabet.
4. **Proof it physically** — printed at three sizes, stitched once, stamped once.
5. **Only then** colour and supporting typography.

**Session 17's assessment is unchanged and repeated: the remaining risk is craft, not strategy.**
The difference this round is that there is now a **specific, checkable construction rule** to brief
a type designer with, and **a mechanical test any proposal can be held to.**

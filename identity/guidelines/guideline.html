The YBMA identity system

The identity is confirmed and prepared for use. This guideline is written for the designers, developers, printers and vendors who will work with it — practical enough to use without asking the founders how the brand works. It is restrained on purpose: three colours, one typeface, one shape rule. The strength of the system is discipline.

Specimens are set in Söhne where it is installed. Where it is not, Helvetica Neue stands in and the proportions are approximate; the design decision is not affected.

1 Brand essence

YBMA builds and runs businesses properly — and builds them to do more than they had to.

The Operator. YBMA is not an investor that owns things from a distance, and not an agency that hands over and leaves. It is a hands-on company that builds systems, businesses and eventually a group — and stays responsible for how they run. That is the strategic direction settled in the earlier sessions and it is the idea every visual decision here serves.

The long-term idea, as recorded: in ten years, YBMA should mean it was built to do more than it had to. Capacity, not decoration. A purpose that survives diversification into technology, real estate, agriculture and financial services, because it describes how things are made, not what is sold.

Origin without ornament. Nigerian by authorship, global by standard. The recorded principle: built for the hardest conditions, therefore good enough for the easiest. Origin is stated once and never explained; it appears in the work as standards and resilience, never as motif.

Fixed facts the identity must respect

  • The name is spelled, never spoken. Y-B-M-A. No pronunciation, no sound for a symbol to attach to.
  • The four founders are not the brand's argument. Four is never counted, keyed or illustrated.
  • The parent is independent of the technology business. YBMA Technology is the first division, not the company.
  • Plain speech. The brand says what it does. No slogans doing the work of proof.

2 Strategic foundations

Locked, and not reinterpreted here: the Operator · Nigerian by authorship, global by standard · the parent independent of YBMA Technology · plain speech · young + serious · professional + human · one identity across future businesses.

Strategy

What the brand has to do now. Win business for a digital-services firm in Nigeria — websites, custom software, internal business systems, automation — against buyers who have often been burned by technology they paid for and could not keep using.

What it has to do in ten to fifteen years. Carry a diversified group without a rebrand, and endorse businesses YBMA builds or acquires without making every one of them look like a sub-brand.

How the identity answers both. A compact, sector-neutral wordmark that never argues for a category; a symbol that works where the wordmark cannot go; a palette that is neither "tech" nor "property" nor "finance"; and a visual language built on one geometric rule, so it can be applied by people who never read this document.

Recorded commercial direction for the first division

YBMA Technology — operational clarity. "We build the systems that show you what's actually happening in your business." Proof points, not claims: the client owns domain, code and accounts from day one; a named handover artefact; a fixed support window with a date; built for Nigerian conditions — light, fast, tolerant of poor bandwidth, cheap devices, power and network interruption.

These are recorded findings from two rounds of customer interviews. Nothing in this document adds to them.

Positioning — Nigerian → African → global. Professional, not boring. Young, not childish.

Against the category defaults. Not a bank from 1985 (heavy caps in a box). Not a software startup (gradients, purple, rounded everything). Not a property company (navy and gold). Not a consultancy (grey, restrained, forgettable). Not "African premium" (kente, gold, terracotta). Not generic-African (green because Nigeria, maps, flags).

For the buyer. The company that builds it properly and hands it over — and is still there afterwards. Institutional enough to be trusted with a group's finances one day; human enough to be trusted by a five-person business today.

Personality — young and serious, professional and human. The rules that keep the tensions from collapsing.

Young + serious — not young + playful

Energy from scale, not from colour.Big type, tight leading, hard crops, high contrast. There is no accent colour to lean on; if a surface feels flat, the fix is a bigger number, a harder crop or an inverted field — never a hue.
One round thing per surface.The wordmark is the soft object. Everything around it is square-cornered. If the mark and the UI are both rounded, the brand becomes a consumer app.
Emphasis is a field, not a colour.The one thing that matters sits in an inverted panel — indigo or ink — or is simply the largest thing on the page. One field per surface.
Sentence case.Headlines are written the way people speak. Caps are reserved for labels and the endorsement line.
Cuts, not fades.Motion is a cut or a quarter-turn. No bounce, no elastic, no easing that settles.

Professional + human — not professional + boring

People doing things.Every hero image has a person and a task in it. No empty offices, no abstract renders, no "technology" imagery.

Ink, not grey.Text is ink on chalk or white on indigo. Mid-grey text is the colour of consultancies.
Asymmetry.Left-aligned, off-centre, one heavy element per composition. Centred symmetry is how brands look safe.

3 The logo

One arch, four letters. The wordmark is the identity; the symbol goes where the wordmark cannot.

What makes it recognisably YBMA

  • One curve, used four ways. A single semicircle of radius 21 on a stroke of 16: it lies twice to make the B, stands twice to make the M, opens upward on a stem to make the Y and closes over a bar to make the A. Nobody else's YBMA is built like this, and it can be described in one sentence.
  • The silhouette. Cup, two bowls, two arches, one arch. The word has a skyline — narrow at the ends, wide in the middle — that reads at distance before the letters do.
  • Every terminal is square. The curves are semicircles, never corner radii; the ends are cut flat. That is the difference between a drawn mark and a "rounded font".
  • Compact. 315 × 100 — the shortest wordmark the project produced, which is why it fits headers, fascias and product bars without a small-size variant.

How it expresses the Operator

The mark is made the way YBMA says it makes things: one module, understood completely, used consistently, with nothing added for effect. A reader does not need the story; the discipline is visible. The roundness is warmth (the human side of "professional + human"); the square terminals and the single radius are the seriousness. The symbol beside it — two solid pieces made one by a joint — is the group in miniature.

What must never change

  • The stroke (16 on a 100 cap) and the radius (21). No bolder, no lighter, no "rounder".
  • The A's bar at 58–74. The A is an A because of it.
  • The Y's stem and cup depth (sides to 24).
  • Square terminals. Never round caps.
  • The tracking (12) and the letter order and case. Never set in a typeface.
  • The symbol's angle (dx/dy 0.5625), joint (11) and sheared corner, cut across the full band and never deeper.
  • Single colour. The logo is never two colours, never outlined, never gradient.

4 The symbol

U3 — two stones, one joint. Confirmed, carried forward unchanged.

120 square · entry 18 · descent dx/dy 0.5625 · turn 86 · joint 11 · corner sheared across the band. Two solid masses made one by a joint: the group in miniature.

The symbol and the wordmark

Two expressions of one identity, related by construction rather than by shape. The wordmark is one module used four times; the symbol is two masses made one. Both are built from a small number of decisions and nothing else. They are not geometrically derived from each other, and the guideline does not claim they are.

Division of labour. The wordmark is the default everywhere it fits. The symbol carries the identity where the wordmark cannot go — favicon, app icon, avatar, stamp, signet, the endorsement mark on other companies' work — and introduces itself beside the wordmark in the lockup until it is recognised alone.

What must never change

  • The angle (dx/dy 0.5625), the joint (11) and the sheared corner, cut across the full band and never deeper.
  • The entry at 18 — the balance of the two stones.
  • Single colour; never outlined; never used as decoration or pattern; never beside the wordmark except in the two lockups.

5 Logo construction

Production geometry, dimensioned. Every curve is a true semicircle tangent to a straight line, so the outlines are exact.

Wordmark on the 100-unit cap. Stroke 16 · radius 21 on the centreline (29 outer, 13 inner) · tracking 12 · Y 58 · B 63 · M 100 · A 58 · total 315.

Symbol on the 120 square. Entry 18 · descent dx/dy 0.5625 · turn 86 · joint 11 · band 23 · shear 12.9 across the band.

From the approved drawing to production

The approved artifact draws each letter as an SVG stroke with round joins. Production artwork is outlined: each stroke becomes a filled contour whose outer curve has radius 29 and inner curve radius 13. Because every curve in the mark is tangent to the straight it meets, the outlines resolve without approximation; the production file overlays the approved drawing exactly.

One optical correction, and only one: the crowns of the M and A overshoot the cap line by 1.2 units (1.2%) so that the round tops read level with the flat tops of the Y and B. This is standard practice for round letters against flat ones; it changes nothing a viewer can name.

Not corrected, on purpose: monoline weight (horizontals equal verticals) — the mark is a display object at fixed sizes and the evenness is part of its character.

6 Logo variations

Five versions. Each exists because a real usage problem exists; nothing else was made.

1 · Wordmark — primary. The default everywhere it fits: documents, website, presentations, signage, clothing.

2 · Horizontal lockup. Symbol at cap height, 0.4 cap from the wordmark. For where the symbol needs introducing beside the name: signage, document headers, launch material, the first year of each channel.

3 · Symbol. Favicon, app icon, avatar, stamp, signet, endorsement mark. Never as decoration beside the wordmark on the same surface as the lockup.

4 · Stacked lockup. Symbol centred above, 0.4 cap between. Square formats and splash screens only.

5 · Reversed. White on ink or indigo. The same artwork; nothing is redrawn.

Reversed on YBMA Indigo — the brand's ground colour. The only colours the logo ever appears in: ink, white, and indigo on chalk or white.

No small-size variant. The wordmark holds to 20 px because it is compact and monoline; below that the symbol takes over. A simplified mark would be a second identity and was not made.

Ink on chalk — the default combination, print and screen.

Indigo on white or chalk — the logo as the one indigo element on a light surface.

White on ink — the night ground: signage, avatars, workwear. Indigo never sits on ink.

7 Clear space

Two strokes around the wordmark. One fifth of the side around the symbol.

Wordmark and lockups: X = 32 units = two strokes (⅓ of the cap) on all sides. Measured from the outer edges of the letters, including the M's crowns.

Symbol: X = 24 units = one fifth of the side on all sides. The sheared corner counts as the edge.

Clear space is the minimum, not the target. Give the mark more room than this whenever the layout allows; the wordmark is compact and reads best with air. Nothing enters the clear space — no type, no rules, no photography edges, no other marks.

8 Minimum sizes

Wordmark 20 px · symbol 16 px · lockup 24 px. In print, 6 mm · 5 mm · 7 mm, by height.

Wordmark at 60 · 40 · 28 · 20 · 14 px. Holds to 20; at 14 the A's bar fills and the cup closes.

Symbol at 64 · 32 · 24 · 16 px. At 16 the sheared corner is the mark; the joint arrives as soon as there are pixels for it.

10 Colour confirmed

Three colours. Ink does the work, chalk is the ground, indigo is the field. Colour comes from the world, not from the palette.

Why indigo. The brief excluded green-because-Nigeria, corporate technology blue, excessive red, startup gradients and "African premium" gold. Indigo is the colour Nigeria has dyed cloth with for centuries — the Kano pits, Abeokuta adire — and the colour the world knows as denim. Nigerian in origin and global in use without a motif in sight. Kept deep and slightly violet, it reads as dye rather than as a bank or a SaaS product, and it is heavy enough to hold financial services and property one day.

Why no accent. An accent colour is the easiest way to make a surface look energetic and the fastest way to make a brand look like every other young company. The founders' decision to withhold one forces the energy into the things that are actually YBMA's — the mark, the shape rule, the scale of the type, the honesty of the photographs. Restraint is the distinctive move: in a category where everyone has a bright, three colours used with discipline stand out.

Where the colour comes from, then. Photography. A market, a workshop, a warehouse floor in Kaduna are full of colour that no palette could specify. The identity withholds colour so that real life supplies it; the hero photograph is the accent, every time.

The palette — three groups

Brand colours: Indigo, Ink, Chalk. Supporting / UI colours: White, Mist, Clay, the greys, Indigo 60 — functional, never promoted to brand. Semantic product status (OK, warning, error, info): YBMA Technology UI kit only, indicators only, never in marketing. Tokens: identity/brand/colour/tokens.css.

Contrast — text on ground

WCAG ratios. AAA ≥ 7 · AA ≥ 4.5 · large text only ≥ 3 · not for text. Indigo on ink and ink on indigo fail — which is the rule that they never meet.

Print. CMYK values are conversions for reference. Indigo must be proofed on press before the first print run and a Pantone equivalent fixed then (near 2758 C — to be confirmed on a physical swatch). Most YBMA print is now two colours — indigo and black — which is cheaper and more consistent than a three-colour job.
Product semantic colours. A product interface needs states — error, warning, success. Those are defined in the YBMA Technology UI kit as desaturated functional colours, used only in status indicators and never in marketing. They are not brand colours and this guideline does not specify them.

The typeface

YBMA uses Söhne (Klim Type Foundry). Confirmed by the founders. Precise, contemporary, quietly confident: a grotesque with enough character to hold its own beside a round wordmark without competing with it. Söhne carries the seriousness the mark leaves to the system.

Cuts in use. Söhne Buch (body) · Kräftig (labels, data, UI) · Halbfett (display and headings). Söhne Mono Buch for product interfaces. Söhne Breit Kräftig is permitted for a single word on a cover or fascia and nowhere else. No other weights, no other families, no second display face.

Licensing — a practical note, separate from the design decision. Söhne is licensed from klim.co.nz per environment: desktop (by number of users), web (by domain, in pageview tiers) and app. The working assumption is one desktop licence per founder plus a web licence for ybma.ng and division sites; add an app licence when YBMA Technology ships a product. Fonts are installed on every machine that produces brand material; documents that leave the company go as PDF. Until the licence is bought, specimens fall back to Helvetica Neue and are not to be treated as final.

Rules that keep it YBMA

  • Headlines in sentence case, tight. The energy is in scale and leading, not in caps or colour.
  • Labels in caps, tracked. The only caps in the system, and always small.
  • No italics for emphasis in marketing; weight does it. Italics are for titles of things.
  • Never set YBMA in the typeface where it is the brand — use the wordmark. In running text, YBMA is set like any other word.
  • Emphasis by weight and scale, never by colour. A word that matters is Halbfett or larger; it is not indigo.
  • Söhne Breit is a cover tool. One word, huge; never in body or headings.

12 Visual language

One curved end, one square end. The rule that built the letters builds everything else.

Look at the letters: the Y is square at the top and round at the bottom; the B is square on the left and round on the right; the M and A are round on top and square below. Every YBMA letter has one curved end and one square end. That is the system. Photographs, cards, bars, tags and panels take the same shape — a rectangle with one full semicircular end — in any of the four orientations. The logo need not be present for a page to be recognisably YBMA.

The rules

Full semicircles only.The curve is always a true half-circle across the element's full width or height. Small corner radii are not YBMA — they are every app.
One curve per element.Never two round ends. A pill is not a YBMA shape.
One round thing per composition.The rest of the page is square-cornered. The round element is the one the eye should land on — a photograph, a number panel, a chart's key bar. Without an accent colour this rule does the accent's job: the curve is where the emphasis lives.
The turn.Elements may rotate a quarter turn between pages or states. That is the system's motion: a cut or a quarter-turn, never a slide.
Lines are stems.Rules are 2 px, square-ended, ink. Vertical rules are used more than horizontal — a stem beside a block of text, as in the Y. A stem is the system's underline, its pull-quote mark and its link style.
The field.One inverted panel — indigo or ink — per surface, carrying the one thing that matters. Fields are square-cornered unless they are the composition's single round element.
Grid.12 columns, 24 gutter, 8-unit baseline. Module proportion 3:4 (stroke : radius, 16 : 21) governs card and panel ratios.

13 Photography

People doing things, photographed as they are. Documentary, close, in real light.

Placeholders describing the shot, not stock imagery. Left: warm natural light, a hand in frame. Centre: a real environment, uncropped. Right: the indigo monotone treatment for background and texture images behind type.

Direction

  • Subject: work in progress. Founders, operators, technicians and staff doing the actual task — installing, counting, fixing, selling, building. A hand and a tool beat a face and a smile.
  • Distance: close. Inside the room, not across the street. 35–50 mm feel; nothing shot on a long lens from a distance.
  • Light: whatever was there. Daylight, fluorescent, the generator's bulb. High contrast is welcome; the sun is harsh and the shadows are black. No fill, no flattering, no HDR.
  • Colour: true. Real skin, real concrete, real paint. Grade for contrast, not for warmth or "Africa".
  • Environments: real and specific. The actual workshop, the actual market, the actual office. If a location has to be dressed, it is the wrong location.

Never

  • Stock boardrooms, handshakes, people pointing at laptops.
  • Flags, maps of Africa, kente or adire patterns, baobabs, sunsets over savannah.
  • "African lifestyle" polish — the glossy, warm-graded, aspirational look.
  • Abstract "technology" imagery: circuits, globes, blue light, renders.
  • Posed team photographs on a white background.

Treatments

Full colour, always for the primary image.The hero photograph is never tinted.
Indigo monotone for secondary images.Behind type, as texture, in headers where the image must not compete. Single ink, never a duotone.
The photograph is the accent.With no accent colour in the palette, the hero image is where colour enters a composition. Choose images for their colour as well as their subject; a grey photograph on a chalk page is a dead page.
Crop with one round cornerat the full height of the image — the visual-language shape — or square. Never both round corners.
No overlays.Type sits beside images, or on the indigo monotone, never on a dark gradient over a colour photograph.

14 Iconography

Monoline, square-ended, one radius — icons drawn the way the letters are.

24-unit grid, 2-unit stroke.The wordmark's 16 : 100 weight scaled to icon size.
Round joins, square ends.Corners are true arcs of radius 3 (the 16 : 21 module at 24 px). Terminals are cut flat.
One curve per icon where possible.Straight lines and a single arc. Icons that need many curves are probably illustrations.
Ink or white only.Indigo for a selected state in product UI, never for the icon itself.

15 Brand architecture

One identity with descriptors. The parent is the strongest common denominator; divisions are named, not coloured.

YBMA — the parent. Wordmark alone.

YBMA Technology — the first division. Descriptor below, caps, 22% of the cap, left-aligned to the Y.

YBMA Real Estate — future division, same rule.

YBMA Financial Services — the longest descriptor the rule must carry; it does.

All four divisions reversed on indigo: the same rule, the same colour, nothing to manage. Agriculture does not need green and Financial Services does not need navy — the parent identity is the common language.

Rules

Descriptor only.A division is the wordmark plus its name in the label style. Same colour as the wordmark, always. No division colours, no division symbols, no division typefaces.
Position.Below the wordmark, left-aligned to the Y, 24 units under the baseline, cap height 22. In a horizontal header it may sit to the right on the baseline, after a 40-unit space, at the same size.
The parent is never a division."YBMA" alone always means the group. YBMA Technology never uses the bare wordmark as its own logo.
Acquired companies keep their names.A business YBMA buys keeps its identity and carries the endorsement (section 16). It does not become "YBMA Foods".
Products.Products of a division are named in text, not given marks: "Ledger, by YBMA Technology". If a product ever needs its own identity, that is a founders' decision, not a design one.

Why no division colours

A colour per division is the easiest system to draw and the hardest to manage: the fifth division has no colour left, the palette becomes a rainbow, and every sub-brand competes with the parent. Descriptors scale to any number of businesses, cost nothing, and keep the parent the strongest element on every surface — which is the recorded requirement.

Rules the site must keep

One round thing per view.The hero photograph, a proof panel or the key bar — chosen, not accumulated.
One field per view.Sections alternate chalk and indigo; never two indigo sections adjacent; indigo and ink never meet.
Hierarchy in order.Label → headline → body → proof, left-aligned, ≤ 68ch.
Buttons and links.Ink buttons, one primary per view; underlined ink links; Indigo 60 focus rings. No coloured buttons.
Motion.A cut or a quarter-turn (320 ms, linear). No parallax, no scroll animation.
Product controlstake the 4 px radius and the semantic status colours; brand surfaces never do.

Grid and spacing

12 columns · 24 px gutter · 32 px margin · 1240 px max; 6 columns below 900 px, 4 below 560 px. Spacing scale 4 · 8 · 12 · 16 · 24 · 32 · 48 · 64 · 96 · 128. Display type scales with clamp(44px, 7vw, 96px); headline measure held by max-width: 16ch.

Logo on the web

Header: wordmark 22 px, ink on chalk. Footer: horizontal lockup 28 px or wordmark + descriptor. Favicon: symbol ink on transparent (16 / 32 / 48); app icon white on indigo (180 / 512). OpenGraph: symbol on indigo or a proof-style chalk card. The symbol replaces the wordmark in the bar only below 360 px.

Typeface loading

Söhne self-hosted as WOFF2 under the web licence, font-display: swap, the exact fallback stack from tokens.css. Nothing rendered in the fallback is final.

Result of the desk test

No mark fails at its guideline minimum in print. The symbol's joint is the tightest feature in the identity and sets the physical floors for stamp (15 mm) and embroidery (25 mm); the wordmark is more forgiving in every process. Nothing here calls for a change to the geometry. The rule where a mark would fail is size, or the other mark — never a thinner joint or a wider gap.

Process notes

  • Embroidery. Satin for the wordmark up to ~8 mm stroke, fill above. Symbol: fill both stones with the stitch direction along the joint; 0.2 mm pull compensation; sample on the actual fabric. Thread matched to the signed swatch.
  • Stamp. Deep-etch photopolymer from the vector PDF. Wordmark 8–12 mm; symbol 15–20 mm; black or indigo, never both.
  • Signage. Flat-cut, no bevel; indigo fascia with white wordmark, ink door panel with white symbol; face-lit only if illuminated — no halo, no backlit indigo.
  • Stationery. Uncoated warm white 120–135 gsm; cards 350 gsm with a solid indigo back; folders 300 gsm indigo flood with a white wordmark.

Bring to the proof

Indigo swatches 2758 C / 2758 U / 2748 U · wordmark at 6, 8, 12, 20 mm · symbol at 5, 10, 15, 25 mm · one stamp each · an embroidered wordmark at 15 mm and symbol at 25 mm on the chosen fabric · a reversed wordmark at 8 mm on an indigo flood. Sign the swatch; record the Pantone in brand/colour/COLOUR.md. Vendors receive PDF masters from identity/logo/, never PNGs.

20 Do and don't

The short version, for people who will never read the rest.

Do

  • Use the wordmark alone by default; the lockup where the symbol is being introduced; the symbol where the wordmark cannot go.
  • Keep the logo in one colour: ink, white, or indigo on light grounds.
  • Give it two strokes of clear space, and more when you can.
  • Set headlines in sentence case, tight; labels in caps, small.
  • Use one round element per composition — a photo, a panel, a bar — and keep the rest square.
  • Put the one thing that matters in a field, or make it the largest thing on the page.
  • Photograph people at work, close, in real light.
  • Name divisions with the descriptor; endorse acquired companies with the line.

Don't

  • Redraw, stretch, outline, shadow, gradient, rotate or recolour the logo.
  • Set YBMA in a typeface where it is the brand.
  • Use the Y alone, or any single letter, as a mark.
  • Add a gradient, a pattern, a division colour, or a second indigo element on one surface.
  • Use small corner radii or pills as "brand shapes"; round every corner of the UI.
  • Add an accent colour, or put indigo on ink.
  • Use flags, maps, kente, baobabs, sunsets, boardrooms, or people pointing at laptops.
  • Put the endorsement on YBMA's own materials, or a division's.

· Record — why there is no accent colour

Kept from v0.2 so the reasoning survives the decision.

An accent is the easiest way to make a surface look energetic and the fastest way to look like every other young company. Withholding one forces the energy into what is YBMA's — the mark, the shape rule, the scale of the type, the photographs. In a category where everyone has a bright, three colours used with discipline is the rarer look. The only genuine job an accent did that nothing else could was product state, and that is solved by semantic UI colours outside the brand palette.

The costs, on record: the instant read of "attention" is slower without a bright; feeds risk sameness unless the indigo–chalk–photograph cadence is kept; and a lazy three-colour application looks like a consultancy. The system depends on people using scale, fields and crops. That is a training cost, and this guideline is the training.

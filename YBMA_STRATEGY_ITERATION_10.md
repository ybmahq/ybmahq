# 1 · Identity design principle

> **The mark is applied, not drawn.**

It should read as something *put onto* a thing to state ownership and authorship — not as a picture
of an idea. Bounded forms, exact edges, and identical behaviour at 16 pixels and at four metres.

**Three rules designed against, from the brief:** no literal nameplate — the plate is conceptual ·
no generic Y, gear, bolt, control panel or industrial badge · the four founders may inform
**geometry**, never **content**. A 2×2 grid and four corners are structural properties of a
rectangle, not a headcount — so they survive the team changing.

# 2 · Logo & mark routes

## R1 · THE BLOCK — *monogram = wordmark*

**Idea.** The name becomes an object. Four letters set **2×2** and cut by a **datum line** that
continues past the block — a thing you fix to something, not a picture you look at.

- **Silhouette: strongest of the three.** Square, split, line running out. Recognisable before the
  letters resolve.
- **Native at favicon and avatar size** — already square, no cropping, no separate app icon.
- **Four** is the grid, not the founders. Valid if the team changes.
- **Risk:** stacked-initial blocks exist. Everything depends on the letterforms being genuinely
  **drawn** — engineered, tight, slightly narrow. Typed, it is a 1970s conglomerate.

## R2 · THE HOLD — *symbol = container*

**Idea.** Two opposing corners that **hold** a name. YBMA is not a symbol you look at; it is a
frame things sit inside. Applied to a division it holds *YBMA Technology*; applied to an
acquisition it holds the acquired name.

- **Strength: the architecture and the identity are the same mechanism.** Conceptually the neatest.
- **Risk: two corner brackets are close to generic**, and the mark means almost nothing without a
  name inside it. Sits near the institutional-minimalism failure mode already on record.

## R3 · THE SCORE — *wordmark-led*

**Idea.** The name itself is machined — scored by a level line that runs on past it. Operating as a
continuous state.

- **Strength:** most energetic wordmark. Best on a building, a vehicle, a hoarding.
- **Risk: no viable standalone symbol.** A scored Y is still a Y, which the brief rules out, and
  the cut closes up at small sizes.

# 3 · Endorsement mark — designed first

The binding constraint since Session 2.

> **The rule that keeps it out of badge territory: nothing circular, nothing centred, no outer ring,
> no rope, no wreath.** Seals are round and symmetrical; identification is rectangular and
> left-aligned. The endorsement is a **line of record**, not a stamp of approval.

| | Unit | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **E1** | Block + rule + two-line data field — *OWNED AND OPERATED / BY YBMA · EST 2026* | **Recommended.** Maker's-plate logic without the metal |
| **E2** | Small block + *A YBMA COMPANY* | The minimum legal unit. Works at 11px on a footer, invoice or hoarding |
| **E3** | Acquired name, rule, block + *A YBMA COMPANY* beneath | Acquisitions keep their name and gain a line of record — matches the Session 3 architecture rule |

# 4 · Wordmark exploration

Four letters, spelled aloud as *Why-Bee-Em-Ay*. The job is to stop them reading as ordinary initials.

| | Treatment | Assessment |
|---|---|---|
| **W1** | Wide-tracked, medium weight | Tracking says *read these as letters* — matches the pronunciation. Institutional, calm, **a little inert** |
| **W2** | Tight and heavy | Becomes one object. More energy — but pushes toward being *said* as a word, which the founders ruled out |
| **W3** | **Heavy weight, moderate tracking** | **The target.** Separate letters, dense block |

> **The real design problem.** The name is a confirmed **spelled** initialism, which argues for W1's
> tracking. But tracking is also what makes a wordmark feel like a 1980s institution — a named
> failure mode. **The resolution is weight, not spacing.**

**Four optical decisions that turn this from typed into drawn:**

1. **Y and A** both carry diagonals and will optically over-space — both need tightening.
2. **M** is the widest letter and will dominate; narrow it or the word leans right.
3. **B** is the only round-sided letter — flatten its bowls to sit with three angular neighbours.
4. **Y** carries the name. **Its junction height is the single most characterful decision available.**

# 5 · Typography system

Engineered, not neutral. The moment the type resolves to a clean geometric sans, the identity
becomes the institutional-minimalist default rejected in Session 3.

| Role | Licensed | Why it belongs to YBMA | Open-source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Display** | **ABC Monument Grotesk** *(Dinamo)* | Mechanical rather than neutral — squared feel, slightly irregular terminals; reads as equipment lettering, not corporate sans. Ships with a matched mono | Archivo |
| **Alt display** | **Basis Grotesque** *(Colophon)* | Rougher, more character. Choose if the identity needs more energy and less gravity | Chivo |
| **Text** | Monument Grotesk Regular | One family across display and text — tight system, affordable licensing for a young company | IBM Plex Sans |
| **Data / tabular** | **Monument Grotesk Mono** | **Core, not optional.** This brand states readings — invoice lines, figures, plate data, the endorsement. Tabular figures are functional | IBM Plex Mono |

**Deliberately not recommended:** Söhne · Aeonik · Neue Montreal · Inter · Space Grotesk ·
Suisse Int'l. All good, all the current default — and Inter and Space Grotesk are the two faces most
associated with generated design work. **DIN** was considered seriously and set aside: conceptually
perfect, visually exhausted, and it carries a dated European-industrial accent.

# 6 · Colour system routes

## C1 · GRAPHITE & SIGNAL — **RECOMMENDED**

| | Hex | Role |
|---|---|---|
| Graphite | `#1B1F1D` | Primary. A warm near-black, never pure `#000` |
| Signal | `#CE3B1F` | Accent. **A machine red** — the red of a stop button and painted equipment, not a startup orange |
| Zinc | `#8C938E` | Secondary — annotation, inactive states |
| Paper | `#F2F0EB` | Ground. Warm off-white |
| Paper 2 | `#E8E5DE` | Surface |

**Why it wins.** Furthest from every banned palette. The red is justified **functionally** rather
than decoratively, and it is the only route that delivers real energy without reaching for orange.
**Accessibility:** Graphite on Paper ≈ 14.8:1 · Signal on Paper ≈ 4.9:1 — passes AA at all sizes,
but use Signal for emphasis and marks rather than body copy. **Monochrome:** collapses to Graphite
on Paper with no loss, which is how the endorsement mark will mostly live.
**Risk:** graphite plus red can read automotive. Managed by keeping red scarce — a signal, never a
field.

## C2 · GRAPHITE & RUNNING

Graphite `#1B1F1D` · Running green `#2F6B4F` · Zinc `#8C938E` · Paper `#F2F0EB` · Attention
`#C8791B`.

Indicator logic taken literally: **green means running.** For a brand whose whole idea is
operating, this is the most conceptually exact palette available, with a state system built in.

> **The risk, named honestly.** A Nigerian company with a green brand will read as **flag green** to
> some audiences — the "generic African" outcome the brief rules out. Survivable only if the green
> stays cool and technical, never sits beside gold or red-orange, and never appears near a leaf or a
> map. **That is a lot of rules to protect one colour.**

## C3 · INK, ZINC & AMBER

Ink `#16181A` · Amber `#C8791B` · Zinc `#A9AEA8` · Bone `#EFEDE8` · Slate `#5C6560`.

The most material route — galvanised greys, bone, amber as the *attention* indicator. Warmest of
the three, best on print.
**Risk:** amber sits one step from gold. Printed metallic, foiled, or paired with black at high
contrast, it becomes the luxury palette explicitly ruled out.

# 7 · Division architecture

**Parent:** mark + wordmark. **No descriptor, ever.** No sector signifier on the master brand — this
is the point of nine sessions.

**Divisions:** identical mark, identical weight, identical position. **Only the descriptor changes**
— set in the *data* typeface, below a rule, subordinate by construction.

```
[▪] YBMA              ← parent, no descriptor
    ─────────
    TECHNOLOGY        ← division, mono, below the rule

[▪] YBMA              [▪] YBMA              [▪] YBMA
    ─────────             ─────────             ─────────
    PROPERTIES            AGRICULTURE           FINANCIAL SERVICES
```

> **The rule that stops Technology dominating:** the descriptor never gains colour, weight or scale
> of its own. Signal red marks the *active* division on **internal** material only — it is a state,
> not an identity. On anything external, every division is set identically.

# 9 · Adversarial critique

**R1 fails if the letterforms are merely typed.** A stacked 2×2 in an off-the-shelf face *is* a
1970s conglomerate — a named failure mode. Highest ceiling, highest craft dependency.

**R2 is the most elegant idea and the least ownable execution.** Two corner brackets are close to
generic and the mark means almost nothing without a name inside it.

**R3 produces the best wordmark and no viable symbol.** A scored Y is still a Y; the cut closes at
small sizes.

**Recognisable without reading "YBMA"?** R1 yes · R2 no · R3 no. For a four-letter initialism
nobody can pronounce on sight, this test carries more weight than usual.

# 10 · Recommended identity route

> ## R1 · THE BLOCK
> **with R3's datum line, and R2's holding logic governing the endorsement system.**

**Why.** The only route that is recognisable without the name, native at favicon and avatar size,
native as an endorsement stamp, and strong on signage and equipment. And the only one where **the
mark and the wordmark are a single object** — which matters enormously for a name nobody can say on
sight.

**What it takes from the others.** The **datum line** from R3 is what saves the block from being a
plain stacked monogram — it cuts the form, continues past it, and makes the silhouette memorable.
The **holding logic** from R2 governs the endorsement, where a container is exactly the right
behaviour.

**The one condition.** This route lives or dies on **drawn letterforms.** Typed, it is a 1970s
conglomerate. Drawn — narrowed M, flattened B bowls, tightened Y/A diagonals, a characterful Y
junction — it becomes specific to YBMA and to nobody else. **That drawing is the next piece of work
and it is not a small one.**

**Recommended alongside:** colour **C1 Graphite & Signal** · type **Monument Grotesk + Monument
Grotesk Mono** *(open-source: Archivo + IBM Plex Mono)*.

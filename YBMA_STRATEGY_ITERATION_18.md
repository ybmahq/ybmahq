# Why it failed, and where it belongs

The Session 17 execution was a meander — orthogonal switchbacks, which read as a **circuit trace, a
Greek key, or a maze.** Three bad reads from one form.

**The mistake was making "unclosed" into an object.**

> **Unclosedness is not a shape. It is a condition.** Applied to a standalone symbol it has to be
> *depicted*, and depicting it produced a maze. **Applied to letterforms it can simply be true.**

And it connects to the other survivor from Session 17: **Y and A are already open letters.** Extend
that to B and M and all four open.

---

# The rule

> ## In YBMA, nothing closes.
> **Every junction is a near-miss — strokes approach and stop one gap short.**

**Why this is on-brand rather than a device.** The parts have not finished joining. For a group
still assembling itself — still acquiring, still building, still adding businesses — **that is
literally true, not a metaphor.** And it delivers the brief's own line: *a company going somewhere,
not one pretending to have arrived.*

---

# The wordmark

**Eleven separate parts. Not one touches another.**

| Letter | What happens |
|---|---|
| **Y** | Already a fork. Now the stem detaches too — **three parts** |
| **B** | The bowls stop short of the stem. **The most closed letter becomes the most open** |
| **M** | Two posts and a free chevron. **No shoulders — the fault that broke the old block cannot occur** |
| **A** | Apex open, crossbar floating. Reads as A only in the company of the others — **which is what a wordmark is for** |

Gap = 6 units on a 62 cap. **The gap is the identity.**

---

# The rule beyond the wordmark

This is what makes it a brand rather than a logo trick — **the same rule applies to everything
else:**

- **Frames never close.** A panel, a photo crop, a table edge — the frame stops short.
- **Rules stop before the margin**, rather than running to it.
- **The small mark is an open corner** — two strokes, no join. The rule at its smallest.

**The open corner solves what the wordmark cannot:** it is solid, so it survives favicon, avatar,
embroidery, engraving and 16px.

**Honest note on the corner:** an L is close to generic on its own. **It earns its place only
because it is the same rule as the wordmark, not a separate idea** — and because the system uses it
everywhere as a frame. **Judge it as part of the system, not in isolation.**

# Assessment

| Test | Verdict |

| **Circuit / maze read** | **Gone.** No meander anywhere |
| "Seen it before" | Open-counter display type exists — **but as a whole-identity rule applied to frames, rules and marks, no** |
| Sector-neutral | Completely. A condition, not a depiction |
| **Young + serious** | **Yes — the best fit of anything in this project.** Contemporary without being trendy |
| African, no cliché | Nothing applied |
| Survives founders leaving | Nothing counts anything |
| Endorsement | The open corner, natively |
| **Small size** | **Wordmark: no. Corner: yes.** A two-asset system is required, not optional |
| **Physical reproduction** | **Wordmark: poor.** Embroidery and casting will close the gaps |
| **Reads as broken?** | **Real risk.** Someone will ask if the font failed to load. **The defence is consistency — *every* junction open, never some** |
| 15 years | Probably. A structural rule rather than a style, which usually ages well |

# What I would say straight

**This is the most promising thing produced in the whole project** — because it is the first idea
where the meaning is **structural rather than explained**, and where the same rule runs from the
wordmark through the layout system to the endorsement mark.

**And it has one serious, unresolved cost.** The wordmark has a high minimum size and reproduces
badly in physical media. **That is not a detail to fix later — it decides whether the rule can carry
a company.** Before going further it should be **tested at real size, printed, and ideally
stitched.**

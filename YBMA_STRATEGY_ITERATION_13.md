# The method: metaphor first, aesthetics last

Not *what should it look like* — **what does an operator actually do, and what is the visual form
of that?**

> **The brief in one line: "These people actually build and operate things."**
> The mark has to carry **agency** — evidence that somebody took hold of something and moved it.

Six verbs tested as roots: **to turn · to join · to accumulate · to displace · to grip · to sign.**
Six different *kinds* of mark — mechanical, structural, additive, effect-based, action-based,
typographic. **None is a monogram, and only one uses the letters at all.**

---

# The six territories

## T1 · THE TURN
**A crank** — the offset that converts effort into motion. *Operate* literally means to work a
mechanism. Mechanical advantage is what an operating group supplies: not capital, but the thing
that makes capital move.
**Risk:** reads as a **Z or an S** at a glance; at 16px an ambiguous zigzag.

## T2 · THE JOIN
**A lap joint** — two parts brought together with the seam visible and deliberate, not hidden.
Every acquisition is a join, and showing the seam is the honest version.
**Risk: reads as construction.** Two blocks and a seam is a builder's mark.

## T3 · THE STACK
**Bars of unequal length, left-aligned** — holdings accumulated. Deliberately not ascending, so it
reads as strata rather than a chart.
**Risk:** it reads as a chart anyway.

## T4 · THE DISPLACEMENT
**A solid mass with a piece taken from one side and the same piece protruding from the other, at a
different height.** Nothing created, nothing lost — **moved.** The offset heights are what prove it
was moved rather than merely mirrored.
**Risk:** abstract, must be learned; the silhouette can drift toward a key profile or a battery.

## T5 · THE GRIP
**Two jaws closed on a workpiece** — the chuck, the part of a machine that actually holds the thing
being worked.
**Risk:** three loose elements collapse at 16px; the symmetry makes it a diagram.

## T6 · THE NAME ALONE
**No symbol at all.** Samsung and Dangote are wordmark-led; distinctiveness comes from the drawing
and the discipline. The honest answer to *"a strong symbol beats a clever construction nobody
likes."*
**Risk: it does not solve small size or endorsement — it defers them.**

# Elimination

| Killed | Why |
|---|---|
| **T2 THE JOIN** | **Fails the property test outright.** Two blocks and a seam puts YBMA Properties in charge of the parent brand — the exact failure avoided for eleven sessions |
| **T3 THE STACK** | **Unequal horizontal bars read as a chart however they are arranged.** Fails finance, and it is the least ownable form of the six |
| **T5 THE GRIP** | **Collapses at 16px** — three loose elements. And symmetry makes it inert: a diagram, not a mark |

**T1, T4 and T6 survive.**

# Recommendation

> ## T4 · THE DISPLACEMENT
> with a drawn wordmark carrying primary use

**Why it wins.** It is the only candidate that carries the strategy as **form** rather than as
explanation. A mass with a piece taken from one side and the same piece protruding from the other —
nothing created, nothing lost, something **moved**. That is an operator, and it needs no caption.

**It also solves the two constraints that killed everything else:**
- **It holds at 16px**, because the notch and tab are large events rather than fine detail
- **It works as an endorsement stamp** without becoming a badge

**And the wordmark does the heavy lifting at primary size** — which is how Samsung and Dangote
actually operate. The symbol is the small-size and ownership asset, not the hero. **That division of
labour is what T6 was right about, and it survives inside T4 rather than instead of it.**

## Why the others lose

**T1 Turn** — the best of the rejected. Fails on one thing, and it is fatal: beside a wordmark it
reads as the letter **Z**, and at 16px it is an ambiguous zigzag.
**T2 Join** — a builder's mark.
**T3 Stack** — a chart.
**T5 Grip** — collapses small, inert.
**T6 Name alone** — right about hierarchy, wrong as a whole answer.

## Risks that remain

- **It is abstract and must be learned.** It means nothing on first sight — a real cost for a
  company with no recognition yet.
- **The notch-and-tab silhouette can drift** toward a key profile or a battery if proportions are
  not held.
- **It depends on a drawn wordmark that does not yet exist.** What is shown is a stand-in typeface.

## What the next iteration must solve

1. Draw the wordmark properly
2. Fix the mark's proportions — notch depth, tab projection, the vertical offset between them, the
   mass's aspect ratio
3. Test the silhouette against the key-and-battery failure
4. **Only then** colour and typography

# Decision required

**One:** does T4 make you say *"yes, that's YBMA"*?

- **Yes** → next iteration develops it: proportions, wordmark, then colour and type
- **No, but T1 is closer** → the Z problem must be solved first, and it may not be solvable
- **No, none of them** → say which *verb* was closest — turn, join, accumulate, displace, grip,
  sign — and the next round works that root harder rather than starting over again

**That last option is the useful one if nothing here lands.** The territories are conceptual roots,
not finished ideas — knowing which root is right is worth more than another six marks.

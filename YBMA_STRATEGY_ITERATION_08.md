# 1 · Evidence Hierarchy

Ten interviews, two rounds, one city. Classified without inflation.

## CONFIRMED

Behavioural, or so consistent across ten interviews that further testing would be waste.

| # | Finding | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| **C1** | **Margin blindness is universal in this sample.** They can see revenue; they cannot see profit | 5/5 both rounds, independently. All five quantified a loss: ₦45k · ₦60k · ₦80–100k · ₦25k · ₦70k. 4/5 named *profit*, not revenue, as their one number. 5/5 had tried to fix it; 2/5 paid |
| **C2** | **The purchase trigger is an unexplained discrepancy — not growth** | 2 of 2 actual purchases. Window from trigger to payment: **2–4 weeks.** Both consulted exactly 3 providers. Both chose on **recommendation** |

Real and directional. Sufficient to act on; not proven.

Cannot be settled by interviews. Only a market settles them — which is why the recommendation
below is a commercial move, not a study.

- **H-A** — A segment exists that will pay ₦400k+ for this.
- **H-B** — YBMA can deliver *less work in, more information out* at a price that leaves margin.
- **H-C** — YBMA has referral access to businesses already paying for software.
- **H-D** — Owners will grant access to financial records. The mechanism depends on it.

## INTERNAL PRINCIPLE

Valuable, and never customer-facing.

- **"Built properly" = documented, yours, and built for growth.** The operating standard.
- **No dependency.** Full handover as a contract term — free to give, and competitors will not match it.
- **Scope down, never discount.** A small budget buys less, never worse.
- **Headroom as a build standard**, never as a proposition.
- **Acquisitions are endorsed, not branded**, until brought to standard.

# 2 · Commercial Decision

## The question, answered directly

**What should YBMA Technology sell?**

Not websites and software — that is a capability list. And not "profit clarity" either, which is the
best-evidenced option and **the wrong answer**, for a reason worth stating: a company whose
proposition is *know your profit* cannot credibly sell a website. It would be a reporting firm, and
reporting firms compete with accountants and POS vendors on a narrow, low-margin promise.

So I went back to what the customers actually complained about:

> *"It doesn't calculate everything automatically."* — the notebook
> *"Reports are limited."* — the POS
> *"Nobody was entering information properly."* — the abandoned app
> *"If I need somebody to come every time I want to change something."* — dependency

**None of those is a software problem. They are all the same problem: the business runs on
information that does not flow.** Data sits in pockets — notebook, WhatsApp, POS, bank app, Excel,
staff memory — and nothing connects, so no question can be answered without manual work.

That is not a reporting gap. **It is plumbing.**

## RECOMMENDED COMMERCIAL DIRECTION

> ### OPERATIONAL CLARITY
>
> **YBMA Technology builds the systems that let a business owner see and control what is actually
> happening in their business.**
>
> **Customer-facing, in their words:** *"We build the systems that show you what's actually
> happening in your business."*

### Why this and not the alternatives

**It is the union of the two confirmed findings.** C1 says the problem is not knowing. C3 says every
previous fix failed because it demanded data entry. *Systems that show you what is happening,
without adding work* is precisely the intersection — and it is the only direction that carries both.

**It lets YBMA sell everything it can do, without looking unfocused:**

| Capability | How it belongs |
|---|---|
| **Websites** | The front door where enquiries and orders enter the business — **captured into something the owner can see, instead of lost in WhatsApp.** Not a brochure; the first point in the system |
| **Custom software** | The core of it |
| **Internal business systems** | The core of it |
| **Integrations and automation** | The mechanism — making existing records flow instead of being re-entered |
| **IT consulting** | The diagnostic front end, **sold as a piece of work with an output**, never as advice (C7) |
| **Future products** | A productised reporting layer is the obvious one, and it arrives naturally |

Every one of those is *making the business legible and controllable.* Nothing is a stretch, and
nothing needs a separate story.

**It positions against the real competitor** — the notebook that records perfectly and computes
nothing (C5) — rather than against other software agencies, where YBMA would compete on price
against freelancers quoting ₦150,000.

**It survives the language test.** It is in the register of the two winning framings, and it uses
none of the words that ten interviews rejected.

### BACKUP DIRECTION — one only

> **TECHNOLOGY THAT ACTUALLY GETS USED.** YBMA Technology is the firm whose work is still running in
> year two — positioned directly against the abandonment pattern.

**Evidence:** C3 (4/5 abandonments) plus both buyers' unprompted *"don't take people's money and
disappear."*

**Why it is the backup and not the recommendation:** it is a promise about **YBMA's conduct** rather
than the **client's outcome**, and it is unverifiable before purchase. It is the right fallback if
Operational Clarity turns out to be owned by accountants and POS vendors — Open Question 1 below.

# 3 · Fate of the 90-Day Reconciliation

## Verdict: **B — MODIFY.** Four changes.

The offer is right and its **purpose has changed.** Session 7 designed it as an experiment with
pre-registered thresholds. You have now said: stop experimenting, decide. **So the offer's job
changes from producing a clean finding to producing a paying client and a case study** — and those
require different designs.

### Change 1 — Price: ₦650,000 → **₦450,000**, with genuine scope reduction

**Why.** ₦650k was an instrument for measuring willingness to pay. That is no longer the job.
C4 says ₦500k+ is a wall for anyone without prior software spend, and YBMA has **no proof yet** —
no case study, no reference, nothing to answer S6's demand for *"references from businesses like
mine."*

**A delivered engagement at ₦450,000 is worth far more to YBMA right now than a refusal at
₦650,000.** And it is not a discount: **60 days of records instead of 90, one line of business
instead of all.** Less scope, same standard.

**Foundation-client rate. First three clients only, stated as such.** Then it rises to ₦650,000 with
a case study behind it. That creates honest urgency and stops the low price becoming the permanent
price.

### Change 2 — Reframe from a product to **step one of the systems proposition**

**Why.** Sold alone and repeatedly, reconciliation makes YBMA a bookkeeping firm by accident. **That
is the real risk in this offer and it should be named plainly.** Reconciliation is the **door, not
the room.**

Rename it accordingly: **The Clarity Audit.** It is a diagnostic with a deliverable, and it leads to
the systems work.

### Change 3 — Make the path explicit, reversing Session 7

Session 7 said: do not mention phase two. **Correct for an experiment — contaminating the test — and
wrong for a business.** A client should know where this leads, because the recurring relationship is
the business model and the systems build is where the margin is.

Say it plainly: *"Most of the time this ends in one of two places. Either your existing tools can
give you what you need and we set that up — or they can't, and we'll tell you exactly what needs
building and what it costs. Either way you'll know."*

### Change 4 — Keep, unchanged, everything the evidence supports

**No new data entry. Full handover in the client's name from day one, as a contract clause. A 60-day
support window with an actual date. The findings session in person, with the co-decision-maker
present** (C10).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **The Clarity Audit** |
| **Exact customer** | A Nigerian business already paying for some technology, with digital transaction records, ≥150 transactions/week or ≥₦5m/month, where staff other than the owner touch records — and where the owner cannot answer *"what did we make last month"* without manual work. Full profile in section 4 |
| **Exact problem** | The information exists — POS, bank, invoices, spreadsheets, WhatsApp — and nothing connects it, so the owner cannot see actual margin and cannot explain gaps when they appear |
| **Deliverables** | 1. **60 days of records reconciled** — sales↔bank, stock↔sales, purchases↔invoices. 2. **A findings session**, one hour, in person, with the co-decision-maker: what you actually made by line, and where the gap went. 3. **A recurring figure** — weekly, from the same sources, **no new data entry by anyone.** 4. **A written recommendation**: what your existing tools can do, and what would need building. 5. **Every file, sheet, logic and account, in the client's name from day one.** 6. **60 days of support, with a date** |

or a long-serving staff member. Frame the engagement as **understanding the numbers**, never as
finding the culprit. An owner may decline for reasons that have nothing to do with value, and
misreading that as a value objection would be a mistake.

# 4 · Initial Customer Profile

**"Nigerian MSMEs" is retired as a working definition.** Headcount predicted nothing.

## The ICP

> ### Nigerian businesses running on partial systems.
>
> They have already bought technology. It answers some questions and not the ones that matter. They
> are too big for a notebook and too small to have anybody whose job is the numbers.

### The five qualifying criteria

| | Criterion | Why — evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Already spends money on technology monthly** — POS fee, software subscription, hosting, a developer retainer. **Any amount** | **S2. The single strongest predictor in the evidence.** The only respondent paying monthly is the only one who did not reject ₦500k |
| **2** | **Already produces digital transaction records** | **C3.** Without them the offer cannot be delivered without new data entry — which is the thing that kills solutions |
| **3** | **≥150 transactions/week, or ≥₦5m/month turnover** | Below this, margin leakage is not material enough to justify the price. The comparable respondent runs 300–400/week |
| **4** | **Two or more people other than the owner touch records or money** | This is what *creates* the reconciliation problem. Solo operators do not have it in this form |
| **5** | **Has had at least one unexplained discrepancy** | **C2. The trigger in 2 of 2 purchases.** No discrepancy, no urgency, no ROI story |

**Size bracket: ₦5m–₦100m monthly turnover · roughly 5–40 staff.**

**This is deliberately up-market from the original definition, and the evidence requires it.** Below
₦5m/month the arithmetic does not work at any price YBMA can deliver for. Above ₦100m/month the
business goes to a larger firm with references YBMA does not yet have.

### Sectors, ranked by fit

| Rank | Sector | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **High-volume or multi-location retail** — supermarket, pharmacy, provisions, phone/electronics | Highest transaction volume, POS data already exists, clearest margin problem. The closest match to the one respondent who behaved differently on price |
| **2** | **Hospitality** — hotels, short-let operators, restaurant groups | Booking systems, shift staff, high transaction count, notorious reconciliation difficulty |
| **3** | **Distribution and wholesale** | Invoicing, credit customers, volatile purchase prices. The foodstuff respondent's *"prices change almost every time"* problem, at a scale where it is worth real money |
| **4** | **Transport and logistics fleets** — 5+ vehicles | Per-vehicle economics. The transport respondent's problem, scaled: *"checking what each driver actually made and what was spent"* |
| **5** | **Clinics, labs, pharmacies** | Billing systems, regulated records, real money, and records that must reconcile |
| **6** | **Private schools** | Fee collection, recurring billing, parent records. `[INTERPRETATION]` Genuinely underserved in Nigeria, with real budgets and real record chaos |

**Deprioritised:** professional services — low transaction volume and a handful of large invoices
means the mechanism has nothing to read. **Excluded:** solo operators; notebook-only businesses;
under ₦2m/month.

### Why these become long-term clients

Each of the top four **needs more over time by its own nature** — retail as it adds locations,
hospitality as it adds properties, distribution as it adds SKUs and credit customers, fleets as they
add vehicles. The Clarity Audit finds the ceiling; the systems work raises it; the next location
lowers it again. **That is a recurring relationship by construction, not by retention effort** —
which is the recurring revenue the group ambition needs.

# 5 · YBMA Technology Positioning

## The choice: **3 — A business systems company.**

### Why, and why not the others

| Option | Verdict |
|---|---|
| **1. Software / web development company** | **No.** Describes output, not value. Commoditised — it puts YBMA against freelancers quoting ₦150,000. And the founders have ruled it out since Session 1 |
| **2. Business technology partner** | **No.** "Partner" appears nowhere in ten interviews; it is our word. And it implies advisory value, which C7 kills outright — **zero of five have ever paid for advice** |
| **3. A business systems company** | **YES** |
| **4. Digital infrastructure company** | **No.** Abstract and cold. C6 says abstract language fails with this customer, and "infrastructure" describes YBMA's assets rather than the client's outcome |
| **5. Technology consultancy / implementation** | **No** as primary. "Consultancy" is dead on C7. "Implementation" survives as the backup direction in section 2 |

### Why "business systems" is the right primary identity

1. **"Systems" names what the customer is actually missing.** Their information does not connect.
   They do not lack software; they lack a system. That is the gap in their own words.
2. **It covers every capability without strain** — website, custom software, internal systems,
   integrations, consulting-as-a-piece-of-work, future products. Section 2 maps each.
3. **"Business" keeps it about the outcome.** A business systems company is judged on whether the
   business runs better, which is the right test.
4. **It is uncrowded in Nigeria.** "Software development company" and "digital agency" are saturated
   categories. "Business systems" is not, and it is plain enough to say to a shop owner.
5. **It scales into products.** A systems company that productises a reporting layer has grown; a
   web agency that launches a product has pivoted.
6. **It survives the customer's language test** and avoids every rejected word.

**One-line identity, provisional wording:**

> **YBMA Technology is a business systems company. We build the systems that show a business what is
> actually happening inside it — and let the owner act on it.**

# 6 · YBMA vs YBMA Technology Architecture

## The definitions

> ## YBMA
> **A Nigerian group that builds and buys businesses in order to run them.**
>
> Not an investor, not a holding company that allocates and waits. An **operator** — it takes
> businesses and runs them better than they were run before, and it knows exactly what each one
> earns.

> ## YBMA Technology
> **A business systems company.** The first business YBMA built and the first it runs. It builds the
> systems that show a business what is actually happening inside it.

## Why this separation actually holds

`[INTERPRETATION]` Most attempts at this fail because the parent brand is quietly the division's
brand in a wider font. This one holds because of a structural relationship rather than a stated
rule:

> **YBMA Technology runs systems for other people's businesses. YBMA runs businesses.**
> **Same verb, different scale.**

The technology business is a natural first instance of the parent idea rather than its source. A
farm, a building, a loan book and a software system are all things that can be run well or badly,
and knowing what each earns is the same discipline at every scale.

## What must never cross upward

Margin blindness · input friction · reconciliation · POS, WhatsApp and spreadsheet mechanics · the
Clarity Audit · ₦450,000 · "business systems" as a category · the 2–4 week trigger window.

`[INTERPRETATION]` *"We reconcile your stock against your bank statements"* is an excellent thing
for a technology division to say and a meaningless thing for a company that will one day own
farmland.

## What must never cross downward

Group and institutional register · "operator" as customer-facing language *(insider word)* ·
acquisition and capital language · the ₦500k–₦2m band as an identity.

## The rule for this phase

> **A commercial win does not become the master brand.**

If the Clarity Audit sells, the temptation will be immediate. Taking it would repeat the error of
Sessions 2 and 3 — promoting whatever currently looks strongest into the master-brand slot — and
weld the parent company to a technology service, the single outcome ruled out since Session 1.

# 7 · Three Master-Brand Territories

---

## T-A · THE CLEAR-EYED OWNER

**What YBMA stands for.** Knowing the real economics of everything it touches, and refusing
comfortable illusions about them.

**Central idea.** Most businesses — and most groups — run on flattering numbers. Revenue feels like
profit; busy feels like successful. YBMA's defining habit is looking at the actual figure, in its own
businesses and in its clients'.

**Why it fits the founders.** It is the discipline of a young group that has to earn everything and
cannot afford self-deception. And it is the only territory that comes directly from the confirmed
customer evidence.

**Why it survives diversification.** Perfectly. Determining what each holding really earns and
allocating accordingly **is the literal function of a parent company.** A farm, a building, a loan
book and a software system all have real economics that can be known or guessed at.

**Why it is credible from Nigeria.** Strongly. In a market where optimistic accounting and
informality are common, a group whose identity is *knowing the real number* is both differentiating
and needed — and it is culturally responsive without a single decorative gesture.

**Emotional quality.** Clear, composed, unsentimental, adult. The feeling of someone who has done
the arithmetic and is not anxious about it.

**Visual instinct.** Precise, legible, high-contrast, structured. Type-led rather than symbol-led.
Numerical without being financial. Generous space, exact alignment.

**Risks.** **Reads as accounting or audit at group level.** Low warmth — it could easily become the
cold, institutional register the founders explicitly rejected. And the serious one: **it is derived
from the technology business**, so it risks the parent brand being defined by division one — the
exact failure mode we are guarding against.

---

## T-B · THE OPERATOR

**What YBMA stands for.** Running things, not merely owning them.

**Central idea.** The difference between a portfolio and a group is whether anybody actually does
the work. Nigeria has capital looking for returns and a shortage of people who will run the thing
well. **YBMA supplies the scarce input.**

**Why it fits the founders.** Four hands-on, technical people who will be **inside** the businesses
rather than above them. It is honest about what they are — operators, not financiers — and it is
true today: they operate YBMA Technology.

**Why it survives diversification.** Excellently, and better than any other. Operating is
sector-agnostic and it **is** the stated ambition: build, acquire, own, run. It also solves the
founder-dependency requirement structurally — an operating group is defined by its operations, not
by its founders.

**Why it is credible from Nigeria.** Very. Operational capability, not capital, is the scarce input,
and the proof case is domestic: **Dangote is an operating group, not a holding company.** The
differentiation from it is generation and method, not structure.

**Emotional quality.** Hands-on, energetic, unglamorous, purposeful. Sleeves rolled up. Young and
ambitious rather than stately — **this is the territory that restores the energy Sessions 1–3
removed.**

**Visual instinct.** Solid, functional, confident, contemporary. More colour latitude than T-A and
considerably more life. Real photography of real places and real work rather than abstraction.

**Risks.** *"Operator"* is insider language and must never be customer-facing. It invites *"what
have you operated?"*, which is currently answerable with one business. And the visual risk is real:
**an operating group can drift into looking like a 1970s conglomerate.**

---

## T-C · BUILT FROM HERE

**What YBMA stands for.** That world-class capability is home-grown, not imported — and proving it
by building rather than arguing.

**Central idea.** A generation tired of the assumption that serious things come from elsewhere,
building the counter-evidence. Extends the principle already agreed: **built for the hardest
conditions, therefore good enough for the easiest.**

**Why it fits the founders.** It explains why four young people would build a group rather than take
jobs. It is the most emotionally true of the three.

**Why it survives diversification.** Well. *Built from here* works for software, buildings, farms and
financial products equally.

**Why it is credible from Nigeria.** It **is** Nigeria — and it satisfies *subtly African* correctly,
because the Africanness is the authorship and the standard rather than the ornament.

**Emotional quality.** Energetic, proud, forward-leaning, warm. The most alive of the three.

**Visual instinct.** Confident and contemporary, more colour, more human. Photography of real people
and real places. The least corporate of the three by a distance.

**Risks.** **The highest cliché risk of anything in this project.** "African excellence" is crowded
narrative territory and it is exactly where *"generic African tech"* — which the founders ruled out
in writing — lives. It is also about YBMA's identity rather than any business outcome, and it can
age into something closer to nationalism. And it risks being all narrative and no substance.

# 8 · Recommended Master-Brand Territory

## **T-B — THE OPERATOR**

### Why it wins

1. **It describes what YBMA *is*, not how it behaves or where it is from.** Build, buy, own, run —
   that is the literal business model, and brands built on the business model do not expire.
2. **It makes the technology business a natural first instance rather than the source of the idea.**
   YBMA Technology runs systems for other businesses; YBMA runs businesses. **This is the only
   territory where division one does not define the parent** — which is the requirement that has
   governed this project since Session 1.
3. **It solves founder-dependency structurally.** An operating group is defined by what it operates.
4. **It carries the energy the brief demands.** Operating is active, hands-on and unglamorous —
   young and ambitious rather than institutional. T-A cannot deliver this and T-C delivers it at the
   cost of cliché risk.
5. **It is credible today.** They operate a business. T-A is claimable but derived; T-C is a claim
   about character that invites scepticism.
6. **It is culturally accurate without a single decorative gesture.**

### And nothing is wasted

The other two do not lose — **they become layers of T-B**, in a genuine hierarchy rather than a
blend:

| Layer | Territory | Role |
|---|---|---|
| **What YBMA is** | **T-B The Operator** | The identity |
| **How YBMA operates** | **T-A The Clear-Eyed Owner** | The discipline. *An operator that does not know the real numbers is not operating — it is hoping.* This is also where the confirmed customer evidence lives |
| **Where YBMA is from** | **T-C Built From Here** | The provenance. Stated in words, never in ornament — consistent with the principle held since Session 1 |

### Challenging my own recommendation, as instructed

**"We build and run businesses" is not highly distinctive.** Many groups say it. **True.** The
distinctiveness has to come from the T-A layer — the numbers discipline — and from actually doing it.
This is a territory that earns distinctiveness through conduct rather than claiming it through
language, which is slower and more durable. I would rather that than a clever idea YBMA cannot
support.

**It invites a question they cannot fully answer.** *What have you operated?* One business, for a
short time. **The mitigation is the rule already established: state ambition as direction, never as
achievement.** Say *building*, never *leading*.

**The visual risk is genuine.** An operating group can read as a heavy, dated conglomerate. That is
a Stage 6 problem and it is now on record as the primary thing to design against.

**What would change my mind.** If the founders' actual intention is to be capital-led — to invest
and appoint managers rather than run things themselves — then T-B is false and T-A becomes the
recommendation. **That is a founder decision about the business, not a brand judgement**, and it is
the one thing I would want confirmed before the strategy is locked.

## Strategic principles — the decision rules

Seven. These are how the strategy operates when nobody is in the room to interpret it.

1. **A commercial win does not become the master brand.**
2. **Scope down, never discount.** A small budget buys less, never worse.
3. **Say the number.** Vagueness is how this market is habitually sold to; specificity is the
   cheapest available differentiator.
4. **Nothing we build requires us.** Handover is a contract term, not a gesture.
5. **State ambition as direction, never as achievement.** *Building*, never *leading*.
6. **Sector lives in the division name. The master brand carries no sector signifiers, ever.**
7. **Never add work to a client's day.** If the solution needs feeding, it will be abandoned — this
   is the one confirmed product law in the evidence.

## One last thing, said plainly

Eight sessions have produced one uncomfortable and consistent finding: **every route to
differentiation your customers described is an operational commitment, not a brand idea.** They said
it themselves — *don't take people's money and disappear; don't lock the business into something it
can't leave; don't make something the owner cannot understand.*

The brand can be excellent. It cannot substitute for those. **Build the company that keeps those
three promises and the identity will be the easy part** — which is, genuinely, good news, because
the identity is the part you can now start.

# 1 · Creative diagnosis — why Session 19 did not land

The founders' assessment is accepted without argument. Four specific faults, each of which changed
how this round was made.

### The idea was verbal, not visual

*"Three of the four letters contain the same V"* is **a sentence, not a form.** You have to be told
it before you can see it, and once told there is nothing further to look at. **A real identity works
in the opposite order** — you see something first and find the reason afterwards, if ever.

### Constraints were doing the designing

Nineteen sessions of exclusions had narrowed the space until what survived was **whatever offended
nothing.** That is a filtering process. **Filtering cannot produce character** — character has to be
added by someone deciding to do something.

### Production came before form

The ink-gain test was genuinely useful and **arrived far too early.**
`grid → geometry → validation` cannot produce an idea, because **nothing in that sequence ever asks
whether the thing is any good.**

### Everything was cold

Displacement · datum · fault · grid · stem width · ink gain. **Nineteen sessions in an engineer's
register** — mirroring the founders' profession rather than their ambition.

> **The brief asked for young, ambitious, energetic, building something huge. Everything produced so
> far has been *careful*.**

# 2 · Seven directions

Each a different *kind* of answer — three about letterform character, one a single gesture, one
structural, one about presence, one a device.

| | Direction | The form | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | **CUT** | Letters that look carved rather than constructed — flared stroke ends, sharp apexes, narrow set | **FINALIST** |
| **D2** | **TAIL** | One intervention: the Y drops a stroke below the line and runs it out past the end of the name | **FINALIST** |
| D3 | TURNED | Y and A as one form rotated 180° | **Out** — repeats S19's error: a fact about the letters, not a quality in them. And the A pays for it |
| **D4** | **MASS** | The word as one dense object; very heavy, fitted tight, counters as slivers | **FINALIST** |
| D5 | AIR | Light, wide, open apertures, letters far apart | **Out** — a setting, not an identity; the easiest thing on the page to copy |
| D6 | KNOCKOUT | Letters overlap; every overlap punched out | **Out** — legibility collapses, and the device dates precisely |
| D7 | HAND | Deliberately uneven, as a signwriter would cut them | **Out** — cannot carry financial services. Same disqualifier that killed Market Rate |

# 3 · The three finalists, attacked

| Criterion | Cut | Tail | Mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| **It factor** | Quiet | **Immediate** | Loud, not interesting |
| **Aesthetic quality** | **Highest** | High | Blunt |
| Distinctiveness | Medium | **High** | Low |
| Memorability | Low–med | **High** | Medium |
| Brand fit | High | High | Medium |
| Longevity | **Excellent** | Good | At risk |
| Scalability | Good | **Excellent** | Good |
| Ownability | Medium | **High** | Low |
| Production | Good | Good | **Excellent** |

**The hardest thing to say about each:**

- **Cut** — the best-drawn thing in the project, and it may still not be a logo. **A beautifully cut
  wordmark with no event in it is a typeface choice.**
- **Tail** — the whole idea is one stroke, and a stroke is easy to add to a name. Its defence is the
  overrun and the weight, which is **a fine margin.**
- **Mass** — walks straight back into the **1970s conglomerate fault diagnosed in Session 16**, and
  heavy tight wordmarks are the most crowded corner of contemporary branding. Present because
  presence matters, not because it is good.

# 4 · RECOMMENDATION — and it is none of the three

Laying the finalists side by side made one thing obvious:

> ## **Cut is the best-drawn object. Tail is the only real idea.**
> **Neither is the answer alone** — Cut is a beautiful setting with no event in it; Tail is a good
> gesture attached to letters nobody chose.

### RECOMMENDED: Cut's letterforms carrying Tail's gesture

**The form:** carved, flared letterforms — a slightly narrow set, sharp apexes, terminals that widen
at the ends — with a single stroke that leaves the Y, drops below the baseline, turns square, and
**runs out past the end of the name.**

**One deliberate detail that makes it work:** the flare is **removed from the Y's stem alone**, so
the descender reads as one continuous move rather than a letter with a growth on it. *That is the
kind of decision that separates a drawn mark from a constructed one.*

### Why it works where the parts did not

- **Authority and warmth.** The letters look drawn by someone. **Nothing in nineteen sessions has.**
- **It has an event.** An asymmetric silhouette recognisable at distance and describable in five
  words.
- **The rule is a working part, not a decoration.** It carries division names, starts layouts, holds
  the endorsement line, becomes the edge of a document. **The identity is a behaviour — YBMA lays a
  line and things sit on it** — which is what survives ten unrelated businesses. *That reading is
  available afterwards; it is not why the mark was drawn.*
- **The avatar solves itself, and well.** Drop the wordmark, keep **the Y with its tail** — a
  genuinely distinctive glyph and **a better small asset than any of the previous nineteen sessions
  produced.**
- **Origin without motif.** A rule beneath a name is how Nigerian shopfront lettering emphasises;
  flared terminals are what a brush does at the end of a stroke. Both vernacular, both fully
  abstracted, neither needing explanation.

### Still open — all four are drawing decisions, not strategy

1. **The overrun length.** Too short and it is an underline; too long and it is a sports mark.
   **This single measurement decides whether the direction works.**
2. **The corner.** Square is architectural; a tight radius makes it a flourish. Draw both.
3. **Whether the tail belongs to the Y at all.** Coming off the A would *close* the word rather than
   open it. Worth one drawing.
4. **How much flare.** As shown it is confident; more is Albertus, less is a plain grotesque again.

# 5 · Stated plainly

> **This is the best thing the project has produced, and it is a direction, not a finished
> identity.** It has a quality *and* an event, which is more than any of the previous nineteen
> sessions managed — but four drawing decisions stand between it and something that should go on a
> building.

**Judge it on whether it does anything to you.** If it does not, say so in one sentence and the
direction dies — that is a legitimate answer and it is cheap. If it does, **the next step is not
another round here.** It is a type designer, briefed with *this*: a drawn gesture, four open
decisions, and nineteen sessions of strategy behind it.

# 6 · Only then, the tests

Production is a constraint, not a brief. Run last.

**All three finalists and the recommended mark survive ink gain** — the fault that disqualified
Unclosed does not recur, because none of them depends on a gap.

**The recommended mark's weak point is small size:** below roughly 24px the rule closes onto the
letters and the gesture is lost. **Solvable, and the solution is a gift** — the avatar drops the
wordmark and keeps the Y with its tail.

---

# 7 · Process note worth keeping

**This round put form first and tested last, and produced more in one pass than the previous four
rounds combined.** The mechanical testing built in Session 19 was good work in the wrong position —
**keep the method, keep it at the end.**

> **Corrected sequence, binding from here:**
> `IDEA → FORM → TASTE → DISTINCTIVENESS → SHORTLIST → PRODUCTION TEST`

# Exclusions added this round

Rotational/ambigram letter constructions *(the A pays for it; the trick is invisible until
explained)* · light-and-wide settings as an identity *(a setting, not property)* · overlap/knockout
letterform devices *(legibility collapses; dates precisely)* · irregular hand-drawn letterforms
*(cannot carry financial services)*.

**Note on prior exclusions:** the Session 19 exclusion list was applied as guidance, not as law, per
the founders' instruction that lessons must not become a prison. **No previously excluded form was
revived** — but the register was treated as evidence about what has failed, rather than as a fence.

# A note on the geometry, first

The Session 20–22 drawings could not be reopened from this account, so **both marks were rebuilt as
parametric geometry from the recorded specification** — cap 100 · stem 15 · Y angle dx/dy 0.5625 ·
tail sheared on the Y angle · U3 on a 120 square. Every variant in this round was generated from the
same file with one number changed, so every decision below is a **measurement, not an impression**.
The letterforms are construction geometry with a carved character; the type-designer's optical pass
is the first job of the next phase, exactly as Session 20 recorded.

# 1 · Wordmark — three studies, one change, two keeps

| Question | Studied | Result |
|---|---|---|
| **Tail length** | 30 · 50 · 75 · 100 · 130 past the last letter | **Set at 75 — three-quarters of the cap.** 30 is an underline; 50 stops short and reads as interrupted; 100+ drifts toward a sports mark and widens the mark for its height. 75 is the shortest length at which the line clearly *goes somewhere* and the longest at which it still belongs to the name |
| **Gap baseline → line** | 10 · 15 · 22 | **Keep 15 — exactly one stroke.** 10 crowds the feet of B, M, A; 22 detaches into an underline. One stroke gives the mark a single module: stroke 15, gap 15, line 15, and the stem drops two strokes below the baseline before it turns |
| **Tail origin** | Whether anything should mark the point where the tail leaves the Y | **Keep.** The tail is not attached to the Y — **it is the Y's stem, continued.** The stem carries no flare (the one place the alphabet's rule is suspended), so nothing marks a departure. Adding a joint, step or weight change at the baseline would create precisely the "attachment" the brief warns against |
| **Tail terminal** | Re-examined against the U3 corner | **Keep the shear.** The three alternatives were rejected on stated reasons in Session 21. Now the symbol's corner is the same cut, and the two confirm each other. 8.4 units across a 15-unit line |
| **Fork height** | 44 · 50 · 56 | **Keep 50** — half cap |
| **Fitting** | Y–B 13 at the cap · B–M 21 (two stems facing) · M–A 12 → 15 | **One change: M–A opened from 12 to 15.** The two foot flares were touching at the baseline — the only place in the mark where the fitting read as tight rather than close |

**Proportions on record:** cap 100 · stem 15 · flare 15 → 21 over the final 18 units of every free
terminal, concave · Y 80 · B 68 · M 96 · A 96 at their extremes · total width 464 including the
tail · height 130. **Minimum size: 24 px on screen, 8 mm in print** — below that the gap closes
onto the letters and the symbol takes over. The wordmark alone remains the default expression.

# 2 · U3 — one adjustment, then lock

### The adjustment: joint entry 12 → 18

The question Session 22 left open — *where the joint enters the top edge, the balance of the two
stones* — turned out to matter more than "a small move."

> **At entry 12 the lower stone begins as a sliver that disappears below 32 px. The joint then
> appears to start at the corner, and the mark reads as one black square with a white L cut into
> it** — the reading the two-mass construction was built to escape.

At **18** the lower stone is a visible piece at every size and the two-stone reading holds down to
24 px. At 24 and beyond the split approaches symmetry and drifts back toward the generic split
square (U1). **18 is set.**

### Kept, with reasons

- **Joint 11.** 9 closes at 16 px. 13 reads more clearly at 300 px and worse at 16 — the white becomes
  a stroke, the stroke becomes the figure, and the L comes back.
- **Turn 86.** 80 makes the band heavy and the upper stone a block on a shelf (Session 22's laptop
  family). 92 thins the band until the sheared corner has nothing to cut.
- **Shear: full band depth**, 12.9 units across — the wordmark's terminal at the symbol's scale.
  Never deeper (it becomes a tag).

**Final specification (120 square):** entry 18 · descent dx/dy 0.5625 · turn 86 · joint 11 · band 23
· corner sheared across the band on the same angle. Six numbers, all either the wordmark's or derived
from them. **It can go into a guideline as it stands.**

### Alone — tested

Ladder to 16 px *(at 16 the sheared corner is the mark)* · circular crop on both grounds *(fits with
room)* · app icon on dark and light tiles · favicon beside a page title · rubber stamp with simulated
ink gain and a rough edge *(joint narrows, stays open)* · blind emboss *(two planes and a groove — it
is literally two pieces, so it embosses as two)* · signage. **Minimum: 16 px, or 5 mm.**

### Still on record

A faint L remains in the joint — weaker at 18 than at 12, present if you look for it. The
split-square genre is real. Neither is new and neither has grown.

# 3 · Lockup — settled by one finding

> **The two share the line, so they must not share the line's axis.**

| Relationship | Result |
|---|---|
| **Symbol at cap height, standing on the baseline, 40 units (0.4 cap) from the wordmark** | **PRIMARY LOCKUP.** Each mark complete; the shear repeats at two heights — a rhyme, not a repeat, because one is a mass and one is a line |
| Symbol above, left edge on the Y's stem | **Secondary (vertical) lockup** for signage, splash screens, square formats. The symbol sits on the axis the tail descends on |
| Symbol at full height (cap + tail) | **Rejected** — its band lands 10 units off the wordmark's line: a near miss that reads as an error |
| Symbol shrunk so its band sits on the tail's axis | **Rejected** — clever and wrong. The line acquires two origins and the symbol becomes a bullet |

**Scale:** the symbol equals the cap height. Larger competes; smaller becomes a bullet. At cap height
its black area is roughly one and a half letters' worth — what a symbol needs beside four flared
capitals.

**Rule of use, revised from Session 22:** "never beside the full wordmark" was right for the Y avatar
and is too strict for U3. **The wordmark alone is the default; the lockup is for the places where the
symbol has to be introduced next to the name** — signage, document headers, launch material, the
first year of every channel — because that is how a symbol without a story earns recognition.
Neither makes the other unnecessary: the wordmark cannot go to 16 px; the symbol cannot spell the
name.

# 4 · Tail — static, then one pass

**Drawn and working in the visual.** The Y stands first. Its stem drops two strokes below the
baseline, turns, and travels right at constant speed; **B, M and A arrive as the leading edge passes
beneath each of them**, with no movement of their own. The line runs on past the A to its length and
stops on the shear. 1.1 seconds. Nothing overshoots. Nothing returns.

What the tail gives the system without anything being added: **the axis** (the line's top edge is the
datum layouts hang from) · **the reveal** (any page, deck or film can open with the pass) · **the
continuation** (after the wordmark the same line runs on as a rule carrying a division name, or the
edge of a page) · **the transition** (the terminal is a natural cut point).

Refused, as before: bounce · elastic · particles · glow · easing that settles · drawing twice · any
motion on the letters themselves. **The symbol does not animate** — it is a stone. **The motion is
complete as described and needs no further exploration.**

# 5 · Endorsement — a structural check, passed

Two moves cover every case without inventing anything:

1. **The line carries what YBMA builds.** A division name sits on the tail after the A, in the
   supporting typeface, and the line still ends ¾ cap past the last thing it carries — the wordmark's
   own rule. TECHNOLOGY, PROPERTIES, FINANCIAL SERVICES need nothing new.
2. **The symbol endorses what YBMA acquires.** *U3 · A YBMA COMPANY* — symbol at 1.9× the text cap,
   text standing on the symbol's baseline. The wordmark is not used in the endorsement line; it would
   put a second tail into a line of type. An acquired company keeps its own name and mark; YBMA
   appears once, in the corner.

Shown on a subsidiary card, an acquisition announcement (reversed) and a document page. **The full
subsidiary architecture is stage 8 and was not designed.** Supporting typeface is a placeholder;
typography is stage 10.

# 6 · Assessment

### Excellent

- **The tail as an idea.** One decision that gives the mark an event, an axis, a motion language and
  an endorsement grammar, with nothing added for any of them.
- **The module.** Stroke 15 · gap 15 · line 15 · drop 30 · one angle. The mark is describable in a
  sentence and checkable with a ruler.
- **U3's outline.** A square with the tail's terminal cut into its corner. Survives 16 px, circles,
  stamps and embossing without a redraw — the first small asset in 23 sessions drawn *from* the system.
- **The relationship.** Symbol and wordmark share an angle, a cut and a line without sharing a shape.
  Neither needs the other; neither makes the other redundant.

### Merely good

- **The letterforms as drawn.** Construction geometry with a carved character; they hold. Not yet a
  type designer's drawing: the Y's arm flares are slightly heavy where the cap line cuts them at an
  angle, the B's counters could open a little, the M's shoulders are flat cuts where an inscriptional
  M would point. **Optical correction is the first job of the next phase, not a reason to keep
  exploring.**
- **The lockup.** Correct and usable. It introduces one mark to the other; it does not add to either.
  That is the right ambition for it.
- **U3 at poster scale.** In flat vector the joint reads as a cut line more than a gap between masses.
  Real material — stone, plate, emboss — closes that distance.

### Unresolved

- **Nothing structural.** The open drawing questions from Sessions 21–22 are closed: overrun **75** ·
  joint entry **18** · the tail stays on **the Y** *(the symbol descends on the Y's angle from the
  left; both would have to mirror together, and there was no visual gain to justify moving either)*.
- **What only production can tell us.** One printed proof, one stitch, one stamp — Session 18's
  instruction, still outstanding, now with locked geometry to test rather than a candidate.
- **Colour** — untested by design until the system is built.
